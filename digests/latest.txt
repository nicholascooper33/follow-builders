No Priors — "Amex Global Business Travel: The World's First AI Take Private with Long Lake CEO Alexander Taubman"
https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=g5TWnUjbeFM
Published: 2026-05-11

Takeaway: Long Lake Management is executing what may be the world's first AI-driven take-private — a $6.3 billion acquisition of the 111-year-old American Express Global Business Travel — built on the thesis that AI transforms labor-intensive service businesses into high-growth, software-margin operations.

Long Lake CEO Alex Taubman joined No Priors to explain a model that looks unlike anything else in private equity: buy established service companies, deploy an in-house AI platform called Nexus, and hold for decades rather than flipping. Long Lake has already completed around 30 acquisitions across HOA management, HR services, and specialty tax. Businesses under their ownership are reportedly growing 20%+ per year, up from zero to 5% beforehand.

The core insight Taubman returns to is that AI in services isn't primarily a cost story — it's a growth story. "Our view of AI is it's incredibly positive sum... we actually think AI makes people more productive. And we have more productive people, you want more of them. And when your customers are happier, you grow faster." By making existing employees 30–40% more efficient, Long Lake changes the unit economics of services: growth no longer requires proportionally more headcount, so incremental margins start to look like software rather than labor.

Taubman also draws a sharp distinction from traditional PE. Long Lake doesn't parachute in a turnaround playbook and exit — their engineers, recruited from Palantir, Ramp, Robinhood, and Glean, embed inside acquired companies for years, mapping workflows and deploying Nexus tooling. The feedback loop is tight because Long Lake owns the outcome, not just the software license. "When you're just selling software and you don't actually then care what happens with the business outcomes, you just don't see the same business results."

The Amex GBT deal is the firm's largest move yet — a company founded in 1915 to evacuate American Express Traveler's Cheque customers from Europe during World War I. Taubman's vision: equip every corporate travel counselor with AI superpowers — faster disruption resolution, near-zero errors in board reporting and budgeting, and tighter customer retention. The long-term ambition is explicitly Danaher/Berkshire-style compounding: own the best company in each service segment, reinvest for decades, and let the platform advantage make those businesses increasingly hard to leave.
