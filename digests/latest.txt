Aaron Levie — CEO of Box
A long, substantive post on why enterprise AI agent adoption will be uneven. Agentic coding grew vertically because its value is purely digital and the task can scale without human checkpoints. But most work — sales, law, medicine — requires feedback loops with humans, meaning agents can't just "run in the background" yet. The unlock is workflow reengineering for AI: agents that scan customer records for sales signals, process every contract as it arrives, or run swarms through scientific literature. The bottleneck today isn't model capability, it's wiring business processes to support agents instead of humans.

Unsupervised Learning Ep 92: xAI Co-Founder Unpacks the Future of Model Development
Featuring Igor Babushkin, co-founder of xAI and now founder of River AI

Takeaway: The closed-source model providers are getting squeezed from both sides — frontier models may become too dangerous to release publicly, while open-source catches up rapidly — and the escape route is innovation and distributed post-training, not more centralized compute.

Igor Babushkin has been at the center of AI's defining moments: AlphaCode at DeepMind, early reasoning work at OpenAI, and then as co-founder of xAI where he helped build Colossus and get Grok to the frontier. He recently left to start River AI, which is making three bets: a fine-tuning/RL platform for enterprise, deeply personalized AI that adapts per user rather than targeting the "average" user, and local inference hardware to free users from centralized API access.

The most compelling thread in this episode is Igor's diagnosis of the closed-source model dilemma. He argues that scaling has genuine diminishing returns, and as models approach critical thresholds of capability, providers may not be able to release them safely anyway. Meanwhile, open-source is right behind, getting stronger every month. As he put it: "I think it's not actually not the best place to be as a proprietary model builder... you make the model too good, you're not allowed to release it. But then open source is just right behind you, getting better and better every month."

The way out for enterprises is to post-train their own models on their proprietary data rather than giving that data to OpenAI or Anthropic, losing their competitive moat. River AI is building the infrastructure to make that local post-training accessible. Igor also outlined where agents will expand next: scientific discovery and mathematics (anything verifiable with a clear reward signal) are the clearest next bets after coding.

He also reflected on the emotional experience of being present at AI's early moments — when getting a model to generate prime numbers felt like magic — and how surreal it is to watch that same technology now affecting the entire world. "It's gone so far beyond those little experiments that we used to do when AI was more of a hobby."

A must-listen for anyone thinking about the competitive dynamics of the model ecosystem and where agentic AI expands beyond software engineering.
https://www.youtube.com/@RedpointAI

Claude Blog: Claude Code now supports artifacts
Anthropic shipped artifacts in Claude Code — live, shareable visual pages built from your session context. Instead of asking "walk me through what the agent found," teammates can open a link to a PR walkthrough, incident timeline, dashboard, or release checklist that updates in place as the investigation progresses. Artifacts are private to your org by default and can be shared selectively. One internal use case: an engineer kicks off an incident investigation before standup, Claude Code publishes a live artifact with the timeline, suspect commits, and error-rate chart, and it auto-refreshes twice before the meeting starts — so everyone walks in already oriented.
