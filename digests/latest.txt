No Priors — "Building an Autonomous Enterprise for Real-World Services with Netic Founder Melisa Tokmak"

Takeaway: AI agents that handle the full customer-to-dispatch workflow — not just chatbots — are quietly becoming the operating system for essential services businesses, and the companies that nail this will unlock growth that was previously gated by headcount.

Melisa Tokmak, founder and CEO of Netic (ex-Scale AI director of engineering, ex-Meta), joins No Priors to explain what it actually looks like to run an AI-native enterprise at scale. Netic builds AI agents that sit between large essential-services businesses — HVAC companies, pet care providers, hospitality operators, automotive services — and their customers. The agents handle the entire interaction: inbound calls and texts, understanding the customer's need, checking operational constraints (what technician is qualified, who's available, what's the customer's lifetime value), and deploying the right person at the right time.

The incumbents in this space have relied entirely on humans — hundreds of call center staff — to manage these workflows. The problem isn't just cost; it's that these businesses often start at 4 or 5 AM, peak demand comes in waves, and the labor quality is inconsistent. Netic's AI agents are available at all hours and apply the same logic every time.

What makes Netic's approach technically interesting is the complexity of the reasoning layer. It's not a simple booking tool — it has to understand what kind of unit a customer has, whether the job requires a specialist or a generalist, whether to deploy someone today or tomorrow based on urgency and margin, and how to communicate all of this naturally. Melisa describes it as existing at the intersection of "understand the need, match it to the operational rules, and deploy the labor."

On hiring, Melisa is candid about a pattern she dislikes in candidates: what she calls "permanent underclass mentality" — the belief that if they don't learn everything or make money within 18 months, they'll be forever behind because AI will subsume their skills. She quoted Martin Luther: "The Christian shoemaker doesn't honor God by putting crosses on shoes — they do so by building the best shoe." Her read: building really good things takes years, and the founders who win are the ones who stay in it long enough to understand the compounding problems.

On what she's most excited about: AI in education. "No one will be limited anymore — everything you want to learn is kind of in your pocket." And she's equally interested in flipping the public narrative on AI away from job displacement and toward the positive: AI showing up on someone's worst day and actually helping them.

Direct quote from Melisa: "Netic builds AI to run millions of real world businesses that keep the world running."

Anthropic Engineering — "How we contain Claude across products"

Anthropic published a detailed engineering post on how they manage the blast radius of increasingly capable agentic deployments. The core insight: as AI agents gain access to real systems, the question shifts from "how likely is a failure?" to "how much damage can one do?" Progress on model training has driven down the former; the latter only grows as capabilities expand.

The post describes two approaches. The first is human-in-the-loop supervision — like Claude Code's old permission prompts. The problem: telemetry showed users approved roughly 93% of prompts, and attention degrades over time as approval fatigue sets in. Claude Code's new auto mode was built to address this by automating safer approvals. The second approach, and the focus of most of the post, is containment: sandboxes, virtual machines, and egress controls that limit what an agent is *able* to do rather than supervising what it does.

One notable disclosure: Claude Mythos Preview was deemed too high a blast radius to ship in April 2026 — but Anthropic expects models with similar capabilities to become shippable as defenders harden critical systems and safeguards mature.
