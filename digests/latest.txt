The MAD Podcast with Matt Turck
"The Biggest AI Deployment Nobody Talks About | Samsara CEO Sanjit Biswas"

Takeaway: Samsara — at $2B ARR, profitable, growing 30% — is operating what may be the largest real-world AI deployment on earth: 25 trillion data points per year, millions of vehicles, 99% of US roads driven daily, and AI systems that helped prevent an estimated 380,000 road accidents last year.

Sanjit Biswas (who previously co-founded Meraki, later acquired by Cisco) explains why physical AI has lagged the digital wave: there's no Reddit to crawl for what happened on a construction site. Physical world data requires purpose-built hardware — dashcams, asset trackers, vehicle gateways — deployed to millions of frontline workers who operate in environments with poor connectivity and high safety stakes.

Samsara's architecture places inference at the edge (dashcams run models locally for real-time driver coaching on fatigue, phone usage, and seatbelt compliance) while using frontier VLMs in the cloud for higher-level video reasoning — tasks that would have required human reviewers two years ago. Now a model can distinguish a defensive swerve from reckless braking, or determine whether a driver ran a red light.

The big launch: Agent Studio, moving beyond their earlier LLM chatbot into multi-step autonomous workflows. Biswas demos a warranty agent that reads service manuals, cross-references OEM warranty agreements, correlates them with detected fault codes, and opens a work order — compressing what was one to two hours of human work to under a minute. He's clear that agents still get "lost in a loop" and struggle with tasks requiring tight latency, but expects both to improve sharply as token costs fall.

The most striking data point in the episode: a large energy utility told Biswas last week that over the past 125 years, they built a certain amount of grid capacity. In the next five years, they plan to triple it — with 90% of new demand driven by data centers. Physical operations companies are as busy as they've ever been.

Direct quote from Sanjit Biswas: "These are not the tokens you're gonna find online. Like, you can't crawl Reddit and find out about what happened on a construction site."
