Simulating Humans at Scale: Simile's Joon Sung Park
https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=lfhFmwcESRw

Takeaway: AI simulations of human behavior are already accurate enough to predict real-world responses 85% as well as people predict their own behavior — and that's creating a new category of applied AI that could replace traditional market research.

Joon Sung Park, founder and CEO of Simile, traces his path from a 2022 Stanford paper called Social Simulacra (simulating a subreddit with thousands of AI personas before anyone called them agents) to Smallville (25 generative agents living in a town, forming relationships, and spontaneously planning a Valentine's Day party) to a venture-backed company with CVS as a design partner.

The core insight: large language models have encoded a surprising amount of human microbehavior from the web and social data. But Park flags a real limitation that most labs aren't solving for — current models are optimized to be rational and technically precise, which makes them increasingly poor simulators of actual humans, who are irrational, subjective, and diverse. "You start to see divergence in model size going up and the performance in its ability to predict and simulate human behavior."

Simile's architecture targets that gap by modeling people's diversity rather than chasing superintelligent rationality. Their validation study simulated 1,000 people across the US population and hit 85% behavioral accuracy against self-reported responses — good enough, Park decided, to offer as a commercial platform.

On the company formation: "Research is an amazing vehicle if you want to do breadth-first search... A company is a machine for depth-first search. You have a conviction on an area. You find a hill that you want to climb." The company's first serious enterprise customer, CVS, came through a paper the buyer read and a cousin who happened to know Park — a reminder that deep research published openly still generates extraordinary inbound.

Direct quote: "I do see an opportunity today to really take the first crack at building the simulation. I would not have said that even five years ago, but that is a conviction that we have built up over the years as we're going deep into this research."

Building intelligent apps for Apple platforms with Claude in the Foundation Models framework
https://claude.com/blog/claude-for-foundation-models

Anthropic is releasing a Swift package that lets Apple developers route requests from Apple's Foundation Models framework to Claude for workloads that need more than on-device models can handle. The integration works on iOS 27, iPadOS 27, macOS 27, visionOS 27, and watchOS 27. Apple's framework handles the easy local tasks (summarization, extraction, typed Swift outputs via guided generation in three lines of code); when a request calls for multi-step reasoning, code generation, web search, or data analysis, it hands off to Claude and streams results back into the same SwiftUI view. Because Apple's framework returns typed Swift values from @Generable annotations, Claude receives clean structured inputs rather than raw user text. The pattern unlocks a two-tier model for apps: fast, private, on-device for simple tasks — frontier reasoning from Claude when the question gets hard.
