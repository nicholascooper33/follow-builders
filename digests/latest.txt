Cloudflare CEO: The Internet's Business Model Is Dead
MAD Podcast with Matt Turck, featuring Matthew Prince, cofounder and CEO of Cloudflare
https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=UN47z_opfmo

One-sentence takeaway: Bot and AI agent traffic now outnumbers human traffic on the internet — and the advertising-based business model that has funded the web for 28 years won't survive that shift.

Matthew Prince opened with a milestone that slipped in ahead of even Cloudflare's own projections. Last fall, analysts expected automated traffic to surpass human traffic by end of 2027. By March 2026 the estimate had moved to mid-2027. Then weeks before the interview, the data showed it had already happened — in the first half of 2026. The driver isn't old-school crawlers: it's AI agents. When a human shops for a digital camera, they might visit five websites. An AI agent doing the same task visits five thousand. Prince expects a thousand times more bot traffic than human traffic within five years, and calls that estimate conservative.

The economic consequence is stark. As Prince put it: "The underlying business model of the internet for the last twenty-eight years has remained basically the same — it's largely advertising. The problem is bots don't click on ads." He predicts the entire business model of the web will need to be reinvented over the next five years. What replaces advertising — action-based fees, API metering, something else entirely — is still undefined.

On the infrastructure side, Cloudflare has quietly become a major inference and agent-security layer. Cloudflare Workers gives developers an edge runtime with GPUs distributed across hundreds of cities, often delivering better performance and lower latency than going directly to model providers. Prince noted that OpenAI's mobile app and Anthropic's Claude.ai both run on Cloudflare. He also described Cloudflare's AI Gateway as a key tool for securing and routing agent traffic.

The most headline-grabbing section was Prince's candid account of Cloudflare cutting more than 20% of its workforce — not due to financial distress, but because AI productivity gains eliminated the need for many middle-management layers. He was blunt about the timeline: "I do think in the next six to twelve months, almost every company is going to go through some exercise like this where they're going to cut a bunch of their team." His case for acting sooner: it is easier for affected employees to find new roles today than once every company goes through the same exercise simultaneously, flooding the job market.

Direct quote: "The kindest thing that you can do for your team is to do it as soon as possible, because it's way easier to get a job today than it's going to be in six to twelve months because the market's going to get flooded."

Internally, Cloudflare reached 93% of R&D employees using AI coding tools. A senior engineer who was initially skeptical took a month to deeply experiment, came back, and reported 100x productivity gains. The team built an internal AI OS called Cloudflare OS — an enterprise agent harness integrated with their ERP, sales, and HR systems — bootstrapped by having 20 people shadow employees' actual jobs via a fake-AI-agent email address, encoding those real workflows into skill files before automating them.

Building intelligent apps for Apple platforms with Claude in the Foundation Models framework
Anthropic — Jun 08, 2026
https://claude.com/blog/claude-for-foundation-models

Anthropic released a Swift package that lets Apple developers use Apple's Foundation Models framework to call Claude directly, enabling a two-tier architecture for iOS and macOS apps. Lightweight on-device models handle fast local tasks like summarization and extraction; when a request demands multi-step reasoning, code generation, or live web search, the app hands off to Claude. Apple's framework returns typed Swift values via @Generable annotations, so by the time a request reaches Claude the inputs are already structured. The package handles streaming, tool calls, and structured responses back into SwiftUI views.

Available now on iOS 27, iPadOS 27, macOS 27, visionOS 27, and watchOS 27. Practical examples from the announcement: a journaling app that generates prompts on-device but asks Claude to surface threads across months of entries; a study app that defines a term locally, then hands off to Claude when the student asks a follow-up that requires broader context.
