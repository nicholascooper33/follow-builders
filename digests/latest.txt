Chai Discovery's Bitter Lesson: Drug Design Is Another Scaling Problem
Latent Space Podcast
YouTube: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=wv53mDmY-k0

Takeaway: Chai Discovery is applying the scaling laws that transformed AI — simplicity, data, compute — to drug design, betting that molecular engineering can become as iterative and fast as software development.

Josh and Matt, co-founders of Chai Discovery, joined the Latent Space podcast to explain why drug discovery should become drug design. Today, finding a useful drug molecule is like finding a needle in a haystack: screen billions of candidates, hope one works. Chai's vision is to flip the paradigm — specify what the ideal molecule needs to do, and let a foundation model materialize it. Think of it as the code-generation moment for biology.

The key insight driving the company is the "bitter lesson" from machine learning history: complex, hand-crafted architectures with dozens of submodules don't scale. What scales is simplicity — cleaner models, more data, more compute, and a tight feedback loop with experiments. Chai has applied this lesson rigorously: strip out the submodules, figure out what actually matters, and iterate from there.

The founders traced the arc of progress in the field: from the 2018 inflection point in biennial protein folding competitions, to AlphaFold 2 in 2020, to today's generative models that can design amino acid sequences matching target protein shapes. What was once grainy and approximate — like early image generation — has become high-definition and fast.

One counterintuitive point: making drug design more productive won't necessarily reduce lab testing. Just as more productive software engineers increased demand for engineering talent overall, more effective AI drug design tools may actually increase demand for lab capacity, because the return on each experiment improves.

Direct quote: "One of our big guiding principles is just simplicity. When you look at a model like CHI one, there are 23 distinct submodules. When you're trying to iterate on something like that, it gets really hard because you need to understand each of these submodules independently. Once you have simplicity, the whole research process and identifying scaling directions becomes a lot simpler."
