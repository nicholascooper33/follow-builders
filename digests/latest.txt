No Priors: "Travel Through the Lens of AI with Booking.com CEO Glenn Fogel"
Published: 2026-07-09 | https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8nj_0wZkbtA

Takeaway: Booking Holdings is betting that the company with the best AI agent — not just the most listings — will win the next era of travel.

Glenn Fogel, a 25-year veteran who helped grow Booking Holdings from a few hundred million to over $100B in market cap, joined No Priors to share how the company is deploying AI across its entire stack — and why he thinks no one should feel safe from disruption, including himself.

The most compelling part of the conversation centered on Penny, Priceline's agentic AI system. Fogel described using it personally to plan a complex multi-city family trip: coordinating different cabin classes, frequent-flyer mile optimization, hotel logistics, and routing across multiple travelers. "It was wonderful," he said, adding that Penny adoption has doubled month-over-month for the past several months — while generating measurable lifts in conversion, faster booking paths, lower cancellations, and higher customer satisfaction scores.

On the enterprise side, Booking Holdings is already seeing AI drive a ~10% reduction in customer service cost-per-contact, while satisfaction goes up. The company is investing approximately $700M this year across AI and platform development. Fogel acknowledged the key tension: token economics. Every AI-assisted interaction has a cost, and the team is still working out the ROI model — which models to use for which tasks, what the long-term lifetime value lift looks like, and when to push agentic workflows to full scale.

Fogel was notably candid about competitive durability. When asked whether Booking's 8.6 million alternative accommodation listings represent a durable moat against AI-native travel startups, he didn't flinch:

"There is no such thing as a moat. There is no such thing as somewhere you're gonna be protected against innovation. Today, we have a competitive advantage on areas. Absolutely. But those can go away tomorrow."

He argued the only winning strategy is relentless product development — continuously finding new ways to create value for both travelers and supply-side partners, while being honest about what AI can and can't do today. The session also touched on Booking's historical parallels to the dot-com bubble, the founder's decision of when to sell vs. keep building, and how the company navigated near-delisting in 2001 to reach $186B in travel booked last year.
