AI & I by Every — "We Automated Everything With AI and Tripled Our Headcount"
https://www.youtube.com/playlist?list=PLuMcoKK9mKgHtW_o9h5sGO2vXrffKHwJL

Takeaway: Heavy AI adoption creates more human work, not less — at least for fast-growing, ambitious companies.

In this episode of AI & I, Brandon (COO of Every) interviews Dan Shipper (CEO) about his essay "After Automation," which examines the paradox that Every has experienced: the company is as AI-native as it gets — Claude Code and Codex running constantly, agents outnumbering humans in Slack — and yet Every has grown from 4 people to 30 since the GPT-3 era, and is still hiring.

Dan's central argument is that AI makes "yesterday's expert competence cheap." Anything trained on past outputs — code, writing, design, decisions — becomes available to anyone with a prompt. That democratization triggers Jevons paradox: cheap competence gets widely adopted, which floods the zone with output that is close-but-not-quite-right, which in turn creates enormous demand for people who can identify what's actually right. As Dan puts it: "AI is trained on all of our outputs... it makes that available to everyone for very cheap. Anyone now with a prompt can use yesterday's competence to solve a programming problem, build an app, or write a piece." The irony is that the experts whose competence has been commoditized are now in higher demand to verify and improve the flood of AI-generated work.

Brandon pushes back with a sharp devil's advocate question: does this thesis hold for large, mature enterprises — a 10,000-person company with established SOPs — rather than for a fast-moving 30-person media startup? Dan acknowledges the tension and notes that such companies face a different challenge: they've optimized their existing workflows over years, and AI disrupts those SOPs without obvious new ones to replace them.

A key quote that captures the episode's spirit: "The further away an agent gets from a human, the less valuable it is. If you just ride the models, you're going to be fine. If you care about leading a really ambitious life, I truly think that this is going to make that more possible for more people."

The episode is a grounded counterweight to the wave of "all jobs are disappearing" narratives. For builders who are actually shipping with agents every day, the experience is consistently: more automation, more ambition, more work to do.
