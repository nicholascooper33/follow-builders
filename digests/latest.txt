Unsupervised Learning — "AI Vibe Check: Lab Wars, Why APIs Might Vanish & Future Predictions"
https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=W_iO8XxgD_I

Takeaway: AI is entering an era of acute compute scarcity where open-weight models may fade, API access itself could be suspended, and frontier advantage is increasingly determined by who controls the chips — not just who writes the best code.

Host Jacob Efron reunites with Ari (founder of Datalogy, ex-DeepMind and Meta) and Rob (partner at Radical Ventures) for their biannual "vibe check" on everything happening in AI. The conversation covers enormous ground.

The biggest shift since their last episode: coding agents now reliably work at longer time horizons, and engineers inside AI companies have shifted from individual contributors to "managers of agents" — context-switching between multiple parallel agent threads rather than writing one thing at a time. Ari noted this creates a new bottleneck: review. "We now have big bottlenecks on reviews and we don't want to go fully to 'my agent will review your agent's output.'"

On open-weight models, both guests have turned bearish. Meta appears to be pulling back from open-source strategy. Chinese labs are increasingly keeping high-performing models proprietary behind APIs. The economic incentives simply don't support open release once you've achieved credibility — and the panel expects peak open-source model count is now behind us.

The most provocative prediction came from Ari: "There's a very reasonable chance that we see probably Anthropic, but it could be OpenAI, suspend API access for some period of time or otherwise heavily limit API access for a brief period — purely because of compute constraints." In a compute-starved world, labs will prefer their own products over third-party API usage, and the appetite for selling tokens to app developers may give way to protecting first-party inference capacity.

The trio also discussed Fable's release and Anthropic's controversial restrictions on AI development use cases, which Rob and Ari called a "gift to OpenAI" — as prominent AI developers are likely to shift to Codex. Rob remains bullish on Google's structural advantages (full-stack compute, talent depth, distribution via Android and iOS) despite falling behind on coding. On XAI and the potential Cursor acquisition, Ari suggested the main motivation is obtaining coding traces to improve Grok's coding ability. Rob closed with a three-year prediction: Anthropic will emerge as one of the most important life sciences companies in the world, with Dario Amodei's background in biology pointing toward that as their next major bet after coding.

Anthropic Engineering — "An update on recent Claude Code quality reports"
https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/april-23-postmortem

Anthropic published a detailed postmortem tracing months of user reports about Claude Code degradation to three separate issues: (1) the default reasoning effort was quietly switched from high to medium to reduce latency — a wrong tradeoff that was reverted after user backlash; (2) a caching optimization bug caused Claude to progressively lose memory of its own reasoning across a session, making it seem forgetful and burning through usage limits; (3) a system prompt change to limit verbosity reduced coding quality and was reverted. All three issues are resolved as of April 20. As part of the investigation, the team found that Opus 4.7 (but not Opus 4.6) could catch the caching bug when given full repo context for code review — a finding that informed improvements to their internal Code Review tooling. Going forward, Anthropic is adding tighter eval gates on every system prompt change and resetting usage limits for all subscribers.

Anthropic Engineering — "Scaling Managed Agents: Decoupling the brain from the hands"
https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/managed-agents

A deep technical post explaining how Anthropic redesigned the architecture of Managed Agents by separating the "brain" (Claude and its harness) from the "hands" (sandboxes and tools). The original single-container design created fragile "pets" — losing a container meant losing the session. The new design virtualizes session, harness, and sandbox as independent interfaces: the harness calls the container like any other tool, the session log lives outside the harness so a crashed harness can be rebooted from the last event, and credentials are kept in a vault the sandbox never touches directly. The payoff was substantial: p50 time-to-first-token dropped roughly 60% and p95 dropped over 90%.

Claude Blog — "New in Claude Managed Agents: self-hosted sandboxes and MCP tunnels"
https://claude.com/blog/claude-managed-agents-updates

Anthropic announced two new capabilities for Managed Agents now in beta: self-hosted sandboxes and MCP tunnels. Self-hosted sandboxes let enterprises keep agent tool execution inside their own infrastructure (or with managed providers like Cloudflare, Daytona, Modal, or Vercel) while the orchestration layer stays on Anthropic's side — meaning sensitive files, packages, and services never leave the enterprise perimeter. MCP tunnels let agents reach private MCP servers (internal databases, private APIs, knowledge bases) through a single outbound connection from a lightweight gateway, with no inbound firewall rules or public endpoints required. Both features are aimed at enterprise teams that need Managed Agents with full data control.
