Memory and Continual Learning: Engram's Dan Biderman and Jessy Lin
Training Data Podcast
https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=aiR7F4jqjXY

One-sentence takeaway: Engram is betting that the bottleneck in AI usefulness isn't raw intelligence but the ability to continuously learn and deeply encode new, evolving context into model weights — the same way pre-training encodes world knowledge.

Dan Biderman and Jessy Lin, co-founders of Engram, joined the Training Data podcast to discuss one of the most underrated problems in applied AI: how do you make a model truly learn your company's specific context, not just read it at inference time?

Their framing is crisp: current frontier models are incredibly intelligent, but they don't adapt to new contexts the way an experienced employee does. Context engineering — stuffing long prompts, reorganizing conversation history, managing external memory stores — is how most people patch this gap today. But Dan argues this approach has real limits. Tens of millions of tokens generated per person per day is coming, and searching through externalized memory at that scale will be expensive and confusing for models.

Engram's answer is to apply the same training pipelines that make models great at frontier math or code — supervised fine tuning, RL, on-policy distillation — to every domain and private context a company cares about. They do adapter fine-tuning (LoRAs, prefixes, sparse architectures), turning raw documents and agent interactions into training signal, and training per-team models that improve continuously on what the team actually does.

The analogy Dan uses is telling: "How do you make the models learn that just as well as the models know the capital of France or how to write Python?" The goal is weight-level knowledge of your company's hiring pipeline, initiatives, and workflows — not sticky notes in a context window.

Their current partners include Notion, Microsoft, and Harvey. The pitch to these enterprise platforms is that agents interacting with your workspace over months shouldn't need to be repeatedly re-briefed — they should accumulate understanding, the way a long-tenured employee does.

As Jessy puts it: "As an individual, taking notes and having sticky notes is a very valuable thing. We should never discard this. But whenever we get back to business the next day, we always have some sort of trace of memory in our brain, some new intuition about how things should be."

The episode covers their architecture in depth, the tradeoffs between externalized and weight-encoded memory, and why they think adapter fine-tuning at the workspace level is the right wedge. Recommended if you're thinking about long-term agent memory and personalization.

Claude Code now supports artifacts
Anthropic / Claude Blog — Jun 18, 2026
https://claude.com/blog/artifacts-in-claude-code

Anthropic launched artifact support in Claude Code. Claude Code sessions — which can span incident investigations, service refactors, or multi-month data analyses — can now capture their work as live, shareable visual pages. These artifacts automatically pull from your codebase, connected tools, and session context: a single incident page can combine a failing test, the function behind it, an error spike from a monitoring connector, and the root-cause reasoning from the session. The pages refresh in place when Claude Code updates them, so teammates see the latest version at the same link without needing to be re-briefed. Version history and a gallery for managing artifacts are included. The stated design goal: spend more time building, less time communicating status updates.
