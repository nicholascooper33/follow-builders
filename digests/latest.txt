Training Data — "Knowing What Your Customers Want, All the Time: Listen Labs' Alfred Wahlforss"
https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Rumft-rsEu4

Takeaway: Listen Labs is turning AI-powered voice interviews into a living data moat — and their next move, generative customer simulation, could redefine how companies make decisions without ever talking to a human.

Alfred Wahlforss, founder and CEO of Listen Labs, joined the Training Data podcast to explain how his AI-first customer research platform is transforming a multi-billion dollar industry that barely changed since the era of focus groups and Qualtrics surveys. Listen deploys an AI agent that runs thousands of asynchronous voice interviews simultaneously, drawing from a panel of 30 million participants — from oncologists to software engineers — and synthesizes the results in minutes rather than weeks. The company now serves 20% of the Fortune 500, including Microsoft, Anthropic, Sweetgreen, and NBC.

The interview goes well beyond the basics. Alfred explains why people are actually more honest with an AI interviewer ("it's a nonjudgmental entity that's really interested in you — it's a very therapeutic experience"), why traditional surveys produce radically inconsistent responses from the same person, and why the audience targeting problem is where 80% of Listen's engineering effort goes. He uses Sweetgreen as an example: even a brand that seems universal skews heavily toward urban, high-income, mostly female customers who know what seed oils are — and 80% of Sweetgreen's revenue comes from that tiny segment.

The most forward-looking section covers Listen's upcoming "simulation" feature. By building detailed profiles from real interviews, the platform can now predict with up to 95% accuracy how a known individual will answer new questions. Alfred tested this by inputting 100 different titles for a conference talk into his own customer panel's simulation — the top pick scored twice as well as the runner-up. In a direct comparison, ChatGPT picked the wrong talk title; Listen's simulation picked the right one. The intuition: general models are trained on the average person, while Listen trains on a specific niche.

Alfred's broader vision is a "human API" — a layer that lets coding agents and strategy agents call on customer preferences in real time to decide what to build, what to say, and how to invest. He even describes an MCP integration with Claude that lets you run Listen's simulation in a loop, generating and testing concepts autonomously. As he put it: "As we get closer to AGI, it will be easier to build things, but the hard part will be knowing what to build. And that's what we're building at Listen."

Direct quote:
"We hope to live in a world that finally works the way people want."
