Training Data: ElevenLabs' Mati Staniszewski — How Voice Becomes the Interface for Everything

Takeaway: ElevenLabs CEO Mati Staniszewski explains how a childhood friendship forged in Poland's badly-dubbed movie culture turned into a frontier audio company doing $400M+ ARR, and why voice will be the primary interface for a world full of robots and AI agents.

Staniszewski and his co-founder Piotr met in high school in Warsaw, bonded over Poland's notorious dubbing tradition — every foreign film, every character, one monotone voice — and decided to fix it. That founding story is more than charming; it explains why ElevenLabs took audio seriously before the big labs did. In 2022, when the rest of the industry was focused on text and image models, ElevenLabs bet on audio being undervalued and data-constrained rather than compute-constrained. They assembled top audio researchers via GitHub scraping and remote hiring across London and Warsaw, monetized quickly to stay independent, and now span the full audio stack: text-to-speech, speech-to-text, dubbing, real-time streaming, voice agents, and music.

Staniszewski is particularly excited about two things on the near-term roadmap. First, emotional intelligence in voice agents: not just producing the right intonation, but reading the caller's emotional state — if someone is stressed, the agent responds with a soothing tone; if they're excited, it matches the energy; if they speak slowly, it slows down. Second, "audio general intelligence": a single model that can narrate, pause, and then sing in the same continuous voice — something that's extremely difficult to combine today.

On the business side, the obvious voice agent use case (customer support phone trees) is already working well. The next wave is revenue-generating: inbound and outbound sales. Deliveroo uses voice agents to call restaurants and capture their opening hours. Deutsche Telekom deploys them for inbound product inquiries. The Government of Ukraine built a voice agent where citizens can call to get real-time information about the war, education help, and safety guidance — what Staniszewski calls one of the most advanced government deployments he's seen.

A memorable quote from Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang, delivered in person to Staniszewski: "Speech to text is technology, and text to speech is artistry — and we are all artists." ElevenLabs now has over 20,000 community-contributed voices, a library Staniszewski sees as a durable moat: quality data, domain-specific fine-tuning, and a platform effect that's hard to replicate quickly.

Anthropic Engineering: An Update on Recent Claude Code Quality Reports

Anthropic published a detailed postmortem on three separate bugs that degraded Claude Code between March and April 2026. First: the default reasoning effort was silently downgraded from high to medium on March 4, resulting in less intelligent responses — reverted April 7. Second: a caching optimization on March 26 had a bug that cleared reasoning history on every turn (not just once after idle sessions), causing forgetfulness, repetition, and odd tool choices — fixed April 10. Third: a system prompt tweak added on April 16 to reduce verbosity ("keep text between tool calls to ≤25 words") caused a measurable drop in coding quality — reverted April 20. All three are now resolved. Anthropic is resetting usage limits for all subscribers, rolling out improved eval coverage for every system prompt change, and publishing a dedicated ClaudeDevs account on X for product decision transparency.

Claude Blog: Built-In Memory for Claude Managed Agents

Anthropic launched filesystem-based memory for Claude Managed Agents in public beta. Memories are stored as files, giving developers full control: export them, manage them via API, roll back versions, and audit every write with detailed logs showing which agent and session a memory came from. Stores can be scoped — a read-only org-wide store alongside a per-user read/write store — and multiple agents can work against the same store concurrently without overwriting each other. Early results are striking: Rakuten's task-based agents cut first-pass errors by 97% by learning from past sessions; Wisedocs sped up document verification by 30% by having agents remember recurring document issues across runs.
https://claude.com/blog/claude-managed-agents-memory
