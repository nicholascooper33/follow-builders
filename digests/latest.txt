AI & I by Every — "The Secrets of Claude's Platform From the Team Who Built It"

Takeaway: The endgame for Claude's platform is a world where Claude understands itself well enough to spin up its own sub-agents, select the right model, and architect its own harness on the fly — reducing complex agent orchestration to just two parameters: outcome and budget.

In this episode of AI & I, host Dan Shipper sits down with Angela (Head of Product) and Caitlin (Head of Engineering) for Claude's platform at Anthropic to unpack what it means to build an AI platform in 2026. The conversation is unusually candid and technically grounded, covering why Claude Managed Agents exists, who it's for, and where the real walls are when building production agents.

On origins: Anthropic kept rebuilding the same agent infrastructure internally so many times that they finally decided to do it once, right, for everyone. As Caitlin put it: "We built products that were agents that you could run autonomously in the cloud... enough times that we ourselves were like, okay, we're done building this for ourselves."

On the model-agnostic vs. model-specific debate: Angela makes a strong case that going fully model-agnostic may be the wrong strategy for serious production agents. "The harness and the model get very paired. You still need redundancy, and you still might want to use other models for things, but you probably do it at the layer of the agent — meaning the harness plus the model — rather than a really generic harness and hot swapping everything underneath." Different harnesses evaluated on the same tasks showed drastically different performance, suggesting there's enormous alpha in harness engineering the right primitives for a specific model.

On what actually blocks builders: Most people expect prompt and harness engineering to be the hard part — but Caitlin says the real wall is infrastructure. "Everyone hits the same problem: oh wow, I either need to keep a server constantly running, or I need to store the transcript data, and I need secure sandboxing and all these things... if your sandbox loses connection and dies, your whole agent dies." That infrastructure pain was the primary motivation for Managed Agents.

On multi-agent architectures: Angela highlights that different orchestration patterns suit different tasks — advisor/executor splits, adversarial generator/critic pairs, swarm patterns for bug hunting, sequential pipelines for deep research. "If we can make the primitives very LEGO-like, then people can put them together to solve things at a slightly higher form factor... and they get much more interesting results."

On the one-year vision: "We want to experiment with directions where Claude actually gets so good at understanding itself that it figures out what model you should be using, how to spin up all the sub-agents. You don't have to think so much about what kind of architectures there are, because Claude is actually able to understand itself enough that it can write itself on the fly."
