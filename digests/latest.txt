No Priors: Building an AI Guardian for Enterprise with Onyx Security CEO Maxim Bar Kogan
https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=QDsbFLEt9ro

Takeaway: As autonomous AI agents proliferate inside enterprises, the existing security stack is almost entirely blind to what those agents are actually doing — and Onyx Security is building AI-native oversight models to fill that gap.

Maxim Bar Kogan, co-founder and CEO of Onyx Security, traces his conviction to AutoGPT — the first widely-recognized autonomous LLM agent — which gave him a glimpse of a world where AI could take real actions on your behalf. The problem he saw early: when agents become capable enough to manage your infrastructure, how do you trust what they're doing? That question was too early for the market back then, but Claude Code changed everything. As Bar Kogan puts it, Claude Code became "the first widely used autonomous agent," and with it, enterprise adoption of deeply autonomous AI tools went from theoretical to mainstream.

Onyx breaks down enterprise AI into three buckets: low-code SaaS automations (~45%), first-party agents companies build themselves (~2%), and autonomous coding agents and assistants — now over 50% of what they see in the field. That last category, he notes, is also the fastest growing and the least controlled.

The core problem Onyx is solving is subtle: traditional security tools can't secure agents because they're built on the assumption that software is predictable. Endpoint security knows whether an action was taken — but not why. Identity systems depend on minimal permissions — but agents need broad access to be useful. As Bar Kogan explains: "If you're not building some kind of controls that are built for these systems, then you're either going to end up limiting them a lot, making them almost much less useful to the enterprise, or you're going to miss a lot of pretty dangerous things that they might be doing."

Onyx's answer is to fight AI with AI: train specialized models that can watch other agents, understand what they're planning, and flag actions that look out of scope — even when those actions would otherwise appear legitimate to a traditional policy engine. The "secure AI control plane" hooks into your existing agents and evaluates them in real time.

The episode also touches on the dynamics of the Israeli security ecosystem, the challenges of training oversight models without access to enterprise data (enterprises won't share agent history with foundation model companies), and how Onyx thinks about the long arc from today's coding agents to AI managing critical infrastructure like power grids.
