Training Data — "Anthropic's Katelyn Lesse & Angela Jiang: Building an Ecosystem, not a Walled Garden"
https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=vPnVTHYplrQ
Published: 2026-07-14

Takeaway: Anthropic's platform team is building a three-layer abstraction cake — knowledge, execution, and coordination — and the next big unlock is "strategies": meta-harnesses that assign specific jobs to tokens rather than just running them as interchangeable compute.

Angela Jiang and Katelyn Lesse lead the platform team that powers both Anthropic's internal products and its external APIs. In this conversation on the Training Data podcast, they laid out how they think about the architecture of Claude's platform and where it's heading.

The three-layer model is the clearest framing they offered: at the base is the knowledge layer — the Messages API, tool definitions, MCP, skills, and memory. Above that is the execution layer, which is what Claude Managed Agents represents today: higher-order packaging that handles prompt caching, context management, sandboxes, and session persistence so builders don't have to reinvent that infrastructure. And above that — still nascent but the team's current focus — is the coordination layer, which they're calling "strategies."

The concept of strategies is the most forward-looking idea in the episode. As Jiang explained: "The true low-level harness is designed for execution. But the next one is about, okay, if tokens aren't really fungible and you need to give them different jobs — like maybe this token is advising versus this token is executing — you wanna start composing these kind of orchestrated strategies that go together." In practical terms this means agent patterns like best-of-N sampling, critic-then-execute loops, and advisor-executor splits packaged into reusable primitives rather than bespoke engineering projects.

On the walled garden question, the answer is clearly no. Lesse highlighted how they've partnered with Modal, Vercel, Cloudflare, and Amazon for self-hosted sandboxes: "Whether it runs on our infrastructure versus somebody else's infrastructure is actually not important to us because the thing that's important to us is more that the architecture of how you put together these agents in a way that will be powerful." MCP tunnels let agents reach behind corporate firewalls. Connectors let third-party products embed natively. Claude Tag is the Slack packaging of all of this — a full org-level harness that surfaces as just another coworker in your channel.

The team also pushed back on token rationalization as a reason to cut AI usage. Jiang's framing: design a routing strategy — assess task complexity and route hard tasks to capable models, simple tasks to cheaper ones — rather than capping usage globally. "You don't wanna stop AI usage. That's kind of the wrong move."

One emerging category caught their attention from the customer side: manufacturing. "We're starting to see manufacturing really pick up as just a category where people are building with AI." And for legacy healthcare systems with no APIs, computer use is being deployed to bridge the gap — automating workflows through UI rather than waiting for modern integrations.
