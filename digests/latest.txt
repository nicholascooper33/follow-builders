AI & I by Every — "Claude Code Can Be Your Second Brain"
https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=in7i-EVDDlk

One-sentence takeaway: Noah Breyer's setup — Claude Code running over his Obsidian vault on a home server, accessible from his phone — shows how AI can function as a genuine thinking partner, not just a writing assistant.

Noah Breyer, founder of AI strategy consultancy Alethic and former co-founder of Percolate, walks through one of the most detailed Claude Code + personal knowledge management setups you'll see. The core insight: most people focus on AI's ability to generate text, but its ability to read and synthesize your existing notes is arguably more valuable. Breyer stores everything in Obsidian (organized with the PARA method), synced via Git, and runs Claude Code directly on top — allowing it to pull from his full archive of chats, research PDFs, and daily logs. For each project (like the conference talk he was preparing), he maintains subfolders for raw research, saved ChatGPT/Claude/Grok chat transcripts, and a daily progress file where the AI summarizes what was learned and what questions remain open.

A standout workflow detail: Breyer explicitly puts Claude Code into "thinking mode" by writing strict instructions in the front matter of his notes — "I do not under any circumstances want you to try to write it. Take this literally. Do not create outlines, drafts, or any versions of talks/writing. Only gather and organize the requested materials." He argues this is one of the most underused levers in working with AI: forcing the model to ask sharp questions and help you think, rather than defaulting to producing an artifact.

He's also a strong advocate for AI voice modes for mobile deep work. "I found it to be by far the best of the voice modes," he says of Grok, praising its tool-calling and research capabilities during long drives. He spent a two-hour stretch doing research on Walter Benjamin and the critique of mass production while driving — the kind of deep, exploratory thinking that used to require being at a desk.

The home server setup completes the loop: by running Claude Code on a server rather than his laptop, Breyer can continue working from his phone through a terminal app, meaning his second brain is truly always-on.

Direct quote: "I think partially because we call it generative, there's entirely too much focus on its ability to write and not enough focus on its ability to read. Its ability to read is incredible, and arguably much more useful on a day-to-day basis. We produce artifacts far less frequently than we just think about things."
