Unsupervised Learning — Ep 87: Gemini Co-Lead on World Models, RL's Next Domains & Continual Learning
https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NQczevdpxq0

Takeaway: Oriol Vinyals, co-lead of Gemini at Google, explains why world models are central to Google's AI strategy and what it will actually take to make them usable — from extracting knowledge from raw video to the deep connection between simulation and robotics.

Recorded the day after Google I/O, this episode is a rare inside look at the research driving Gemini's development. Vinyals has spent his career at the frontier of multimodal learning, and host Jacob (Unsupervised Learning) uses the conversation to unpack the ideas behind Google's I/O announcements.

On world models: Vinyals distinguishes between two notions — the classical representation-learning sense (compressing video into abstract concepts) and the interactive sense that Omni demonstrates, where the model acts as "a renderer of the world that you can really just change by language." He's candid that the pure transfer from raw video to conceptual understanding — training on all video ever produced and extracting meaning without text labels — hasn't been achieved yet: "That pure transfer is, I think, one of the core quest of machine learning for the last decade plus."

On the multimodal "GPT moment": Vinyals thinks it hasn't happened yet for video and images. Language got its GPT moment because humans wrote so much about the world and put it on the internet. Video is different — we haven't explicitly described or transcribed most of what exists. The challenge is linking visual concepts to meaning without the explicit language bridge. He sees discrete representation learning as a possible path, but notes it's still in early research stages.

On robotics: There's a direct connection. If robots generate data that makes it into the training mix, the world model improves the robot's ability to predict before acting. Simulation and real-world robot data are increasingly complementary, not competing.

On memory and reasoning: Vinyals sees memory advances over the next few years as analogous to what reasoning advances looked like recently — compounding improvements that will quietly transform what agents can do.

Direct quote: "The issue there is linking language, or these concepts, as we sometimes call them, to what you see in the image without the explicit language linkage is fairly tricky."

Claude Blog — New in Claude Managed Agents: dreaming, outcomes, and multiagent orchestration
https://claude.com/blog/new-in-claude-managed-agents

Anthropic launched three significant additions to Claude Managed Agents:

Dreaming: A scheduled process that reviews past agent sessions, extracts patterns, and curates memory so agents improve over time without human steering. You control how much autonomy dreaming has — it can update memory automatically or surface changes for review first. Harvey (legal AI) saw completion rates go up ~6x in testing after enabling dreaming.

Outcomes: You write a rubric for what success looks like, and a separate grader evaluates the agent's output in its own context window — so it isn't influenced by the agent's reasoning. If something's off, the grader pinpoints it and the agent takes another pass. In testing, outcomes improved task success by up to 10 points over standard prompting loops, with the biggest gains on the hardest problems. Netflix is using multiagent orchestration to analyze logs from hundreds of builds in parallel and surface only the patterns worth acting on.

Multiagent orchestration: A lead agent breaks complex jobs into pieces and delegates each to a specialist with its own model, prompt, and tools. Specialists work in parallel on a shared filesystem. Every step is traceable in the Claude Console — which agent did what, in what order, and why. Spiral by Every uses this to power their writing agent: a Haiku lead agent fields requests and delegates drafting to parallel Opus subagents, with outcomes scoring each draft against editorial principles before it ships.
