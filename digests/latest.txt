The MAD Podcast with Matt Turck — "Stripe's AI Chief: How AI Agents Will Buy, Sell, and Pay"
Published: July 9, 2026
https://www.youtube.com/@DataDrivenNYC/videos

Takeaway: Stripe's head of data and AI, Emily Sense, argues that agentic commerce is already live infrastructure — not just a concept — and the next frontier is agents that don't just buy on your behalf but run entire micro-businesses end to end.

The conversation covers the full spectrum of how agentic commerce plays out today. At one end, fully autonomous agents discover a service, negotiate pricing, and execute a transaction with no human in the loop. At the other, consumers use AI surfaces (ChatGPT, Gemini, Copilot) to discover products and then press a buy button themselves — the agent handles the checkout flow. Emily says the industry is mostly hovering around "Level 2" right now, where humans still make the final selection decision but lean on AI heavily to surface and filter options.

Stripe has been building the infrastructure layer for both ends of that spectrum. The Agentic Commerce Protocol (ACP), co-created with OpenAI, is a standardized way for businesses to expose their product catalog and prices to any AI agent — once, without needing to register separately with every model provider. Brands like Best Buy, Coach, URBN, Kate Spade, and platforms like Shopify, Wix, and BigCommerce have already adopted it. On the payments side, Stripe's new Shared Payment Token lets agents transact without ever seeing a user's actual card credentials — the token encodes exactly which merchants can be charged, for how much, in which currency, and for how long. Link Wallet (Stripe's consumer wallet with 300 million users) is being repositioned as the wallet for agents, giving consumers fine-grained spending guardrails rather than handing agents a blank check.

One of the most underreported topics Emily flags is token theft: more than one in six signups at AI companies are fraudulent actors trying to steal compute tokens rather than money or credentials. She also notes that per-seat SaaS pricing is breaking — AI costs scale with compute usage, not headcount — forcing a rethink of how software is monetized.

On the macro picture, Emily points to a surge in US solopreneurs (5 million people now making their living from solo companies, hundreds of thousands clearing $1M/year) as evidence that AI is already reshaping business formation. The longer-term vision is agents as full economic actors — not just buying, but selling, provisioning infrastructure, and operating as micro-firms.

Direct quote from Emily Sense:

"It's not Emily permissioning an agent to buy on her behalf. It's Emily has an agent who's tasked with running a business, and that includes buying some things and selling some things and making some profits. And that'll be the world that I would like to be talking about twelve months from now."
