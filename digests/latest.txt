Swyx — AI builder and co-host of Latent Space, affiliated with Cognition and Temporal

A new paper from researchers at CMU is raising uncomfortable questions about frontier AI benchmarks. Swyx highlights a finding buried in the RLM paper: even without directly training on test sets, labs can "basically cheat by training on test lookalikes, enabling you to goalseek almost any benchmark number you want." What makes this especially tricky is that open-weight models are rarely released with the datasets or RL environments used to train them, leaving little way to verify benchmark integrity. The paper proposes applying standard NLP distance metrics to hidden trajectories as a first step toward accountability — though Swyx notes there is no ultimate solution yet.

No Priors — Travel Through the Lens of AI with Booking.com CEO Glenn Fogel
https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8nj_0wZkbtA

Takeaway: Glenn Fogel argues that AI is not a threat to travel platforms but a once-in-a-generation tool to deliver what the industry has always promised — genuinely personalized, friction-free travel planning at scale.

Fogel joined Priceline in 2000 when its market cap had already collapsed from $30 billion to a few hundred million, and has watched it grow to a $180 billion company over 27 years. That long arc informs how he thinks about the current AI moment: he sees clear parallels to the late-90s internet boom, including the wave of companies that promised to disintermediate established players and mostly did not. "If you think you're just gonna come in and do this business and knock away these very big players," Fogel said, "I'd say you should really understand what the business is before you decide to commit your capital."

On agentic AI, Fogel is bullish but measured. Booking Holdings has already deployed Penny, Priceline's agentic travel assistant, and he described using it himself to plan a complex multi-city family trip — managing cabin splits, frequent flyer miles, hotel timing, and routing across multiple airlines — in a way that no human travel agent could efficiently replicate. Early results are notable: Penny adoption has doubled month-over-month, with measurable lifts in booking conversion and customer satisfaction, while customer service costs per contact are down roughly 10%. The caveat is that absolute volume remains small against Booking's $186 billion in annual travel processed, and Fogel is disciplined about understanding the token economics before scaling it further.

The conversation also covered the job displacement question frankly. Fogel recalled how machine translation eliminated entire teams of multilingual customer service staff at booking.com by 2010, and worries the current transition is moving faster than retraining programs can absorb — particularly for workers who cannot easily pivot, like truck drivers facing automation. His prescription is corporate-level upskilling investment, not government programs, which he views as having a poor track record. The broader warning: if fear of AI causes parts of society to reject it, that is a competitive disadvantage relative to countries that will not hesitate.

Direct quote: "There is no such thing as a moat. There is no such thing as somewhere you're gonna be protected against innovation. Today, we have a competitive advantage on areas. Absolutely. But those can go away tomorrow."
