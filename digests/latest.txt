No Priors — "Building an AI Guardian for Enterprise with Onyx Security CEO Maxim Bar Kogan"
https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=QDsbFLEt9ro

Takeaway: As autonomous agents proliferate inside enterprises, a new category of AI security is emerging — not to protect against external hackers, but to govern what the AI agents themselves are doing.

Maxim Bar Kogan is the co-founder and CEO of Onyx Security, an Israel-based startup building AI agents that watch other AI agents. The conversation with No Priors host Adam covers the origin story, the product architecture, and a sweeping thesis on AI safety as an enterprise infrastructure problem.

Onyx started when Bar Kogan became fixated on a question AutoGPT raised back in 2023: if models eventually become smarter than us, how do you oversee something more capable than yourself? That question felt premature at the time, but the market caught up — Claude Code, Copilot, and OpenCLO drove enterprise adoption of very autonomous agents faster than anyone expected, and suddenly Onyx had inbound from Fortune 10 companies.

The core product is a "secure AI control plane" — a system that connects to all the AI agents running inside an enterprise and monitors their actions in real time. The key technical challenge is doing this without ruinous latency and cost. Onyx's solution: train small, highly specialized models that only need to answer one question — "should a smarter agent look at this?" When those small models flag something, a more capable agent investigates. When they don't, everything runs fast and cheap.

Bar Kogan offered a striking breakdown of where enterprise AI sits today: roughly 50% of agent activity comes from autonomous coding agents like Claude Code, about 45% from low-code automation platforms, and just 2% from first-party agents companies build themselves. He expects autonomous agents to keep growing fastest, and — critically — notes that most of them arrive "without any controls."

On why enterprises are willing to trust a two-year-old startup with their AI governance: "Their pain is so strong that they're gonna say, oh my god, I just saw this company come out of stealth, but it's a problem that I have daily. So, I'll give them a call."

The episode also covers Mythos-level vulnerability finding (the "plummeting cost" of AI-assisted exploit discovery), why traditional identity and endpoint security tools are poorly suited to governing agents, and what mechanistic interpretability might eventually contribute to AI oversight. For anyone building agents in production or thinking seriously about enterprise AI risk, this is required listening.

Claude Blog — "Building intelligent apps for Apple platforms with Claude in the Foundation Models framework"
https://claude.com/blog/claude-for-foundation-models

Anthropic released a new Swift package that connects Apple's Foundation Models framework to Claude, enabling developers to seamlessly hand off from on-device models to Claude for more demanding tasks. Apple's framework already lets developers tap on-device models with as few as three lines of Swift code — useful for fast, local tasks like summarization or extraction. The new integration means that when a task requires multi-step reasoning, code generation, or a live web search, the app can hand off to Claude and stream the response back into the same view. Because Apple's framework returns typed Swift values via @Generable annotations, inputs arrive at the Claude API call already structured — no raw text parsing required. The package handles streaming, tool calls, and structured responses back into SwiftUI. It works on iOS 27, iPadOS 27, macOS 27, visionOS 27, and watchOS 27, launching tomorrow.
