Thibault Sottiaux — Codex & ChatGPT, OpenAI

OpenAI reset usage limits for all ChatGPT Work and Codex subscribers after discovering that GPT-5.6 Sol was consuming far more tokens than expected. Sol is significantly more agentic than previous models — it willingly runs longer tasks, fires parallel tool calls, and coordinates complex subagent workflows — all of which drove up token usage unevenly, hitting power users hardest while median usage looked fine. The team shipped several efficiency improvements and says usage should now last ~18% longer during typical sessions, with the five-hour Codex limit also being restored. In a follow-up, Sottiaux also announced a new open-source CLI and TypeScript SDK for finding, validating, and fixing security vulnerabilities in code — supporting repository scans, change reviews, finding tracking over time, and CI integration.

Swyx — AI engineer, smol.ai / Latent Space

Swyx shared a sharp take on the current hiring landscape: it's a massive bull market for AI-native individual contributors and player-coaches, and a bear market for "heads of X" managers. Dan Shipper — CEO, Every

Shipper wrote what he calls the definitive history of Codex — based on hours of in-depth interviews with the team — without once touching a keyboard or mouse. He did the entire piece: organizing interviews, building a timeline, drafting, editing, and revising, all via ChatGPT for Work's voice mode. He described it as "absolutely goated for creative work." The full piece will be published on Every in the coming weeks, and this is worth watching as a real-world demonstration of how voice-first AI workflows are becoming viable for long-form professional writing.
