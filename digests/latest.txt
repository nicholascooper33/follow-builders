Aaron Levie — CEO, Box
Two must-reads today from Aaron on AI regulation and infrastructure strategy.

On the Fable export control saga: Aaron argues this is "actually net positive to regulation discourse" — a useful preview of what AI governance looks like when it targets the model layer rather than specific applications. The problem is that any government review process involves months of subjective debate over what a model is capable of, and blocking frontier intelligence today, had the same framework existed three years ago, would have left us stuck at GPT-4. His conclusion: regulate the applied use of AI (fraud, biowarfare, cyber attacks), not the model itself.

No Priors — Biohub: The Future of Biology is Open-Source
with Mark Zuckerberg, Priscilla Chan & Alex Rives (Head of Science, Biohub)
Published: June 10, 2026

Takeaway: Chan Zuckerberg Initiative is betting $500M that the bottleneck to curing disease is data and tooling — not funding or intent — and that open-source AI is the fastest path to breaking it.

Mark Zuckerberg and Priscilla Chan have been funding Biohub for about a decade, starting from a question that got them laughed out of rooms by Nobel laureates: could science collectively cure, prevent, and manage all disease by the end of the century? What those scientists finally told them was revealing — the real obstacles aren't funding gaps, they're structural: science happens in silos, information gets locked up after publication, and tools built by one postdoc disappear when they graduate. Biohub's original design was to fix exactly that by embedding engineers alongside scientists and building shared, long-term tooling across universities.

That infrastructure bet has now evolved into something far more ambitious. The new $500M Virtual Biology Initiative pairs frontier AI research directly with frontier wet-lab biology in a single integrated effort. Priscilla explained the key inflection point: when large language models proved they could extract meaning from massive unstructured datasets, the team realized biology could undergo the same transformation — shifting from a "discovery-based science" to an "engineering-based science" where cellular behavior could be systematically understood and eventually designed.

Alex Rives, who previously co-founded Evolutionary Scale (an AI protein modeling company), joined Biohub because he saw it as the right place to build at the intersection: frontier AI and frontier biology put into a feedback loop, where models learn from biology experiments, and biology experiments are designed by what the models reveal. The approach is deliberately hierarchical — start by modeling proteins (they've already predicted structures for over 1.1 billion proteins), then cells, then full biological systems — because you can't model cellular behavior without first understanding protein interactions.

A defining strategic difference from most biotech: all data, tools, and models are open-source by design. Earlier Biohub tool CellxGene — built to help scientists annotate single-cell RNA data — grew into a massive community-contributed corpus that now powers transcriptomic models the team had nothing to do with creating. That compounding, community flywheel is the template for everything they're building now.

Direct quote: "We wanna understand how biology works. I wanna understand the genetics of this person. I wanna understand the risks they have to different illnesses. My goal is to be able to treat the individual as an individual, understand the mechanisms, and be able to intervene." — Priscilla Chan
