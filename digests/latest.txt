Training Data — "Factory's Matan Grinberg: The Coming 'Dark Factory' Where Software Builds Itself"
Published: 2026-07-21 | https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ZesOukBjPmI

Takeaway: Factory CEO Matan Grinberg argues we're heading toward a "dark factory" — software that builds itself autonomously, around the clock, without anyone at the controls — and explains why a model-agnostic harness beats a model-native one every time.

Grinberg started Factory in April 2023, when enterprises weren't even ready for GitHub Copilot. He describes the first two years as "the journey in the desert" — chasing fully autonomous agents before developers had the behavioral change or model quality to support them. At one point, Factory had nearly $2M in ARR but realized the product wasn't genuinely good, so they gave every customer their money back. The core principle: "create obsessed customers," not just satisfied ones.

The breakthrough came in September 2025 with the Droid CLI, which met developers where they were rather than demanding a behavioral step change upfront. The key insight Grinberg shares: a harness trained on multiple models outperforms a harness tuned to a single one — just as a model trained on the whole Internet beats one trained on your personal data. Factory saw this empirically: every new model from Anthropic or OpenAI performed better on benchmarks inside Droid than inside Claude Code or Codex, because Droid's model-agnostic design avoided overfitting to any one model's quirks.

On the current market: enterprises went through "token maxing" (use Opus for everything, including asking about the weather), and are now moving toward intelligent routing. Factory's router lets companies dynamically allocate different models to different tasks, teams, and cost profiles — in natural language. Open-weight models now account for a double-digit percentage of Factory's enterprise token volume, up from near zero at the start of the year. Grinberg predicts open models will take a vast majority of token share over time since they provide optionality and cost savings, while frontier models remain critical for high-leverage decisions.

Looking 12–24 months out, Grinberg expects 90% of tokens to be asynchronous — droids running autonomously in the background, detecting customer signals and generating first-pass solutions without a human kicking them off first. That is the "dark factory" vision: software building software, lights off.

Direct quote: "If you build a harness that supports different models, that harness will be better. What data is to a model, models are to a harness."

Claude Blog — "Building intelligent apps for Apple platforms with Claude in the Foundation Models framework"
https://claude.com/blog/claude-for-foundation-models

Anthropic released a new Swift package that lets Apple developers use Apple's Foundation Models framework to call Claude for complex tasks. The framework already lets developers tap into on-device models for quick jobs like summarization or extraction — now they can hand off to Claude when the task requires multi-step reasoning, code generation, web search, or data analysis via code execution, all within the same user experience. Claude returns structured responses through SwiftUI views via streaming. The package handles the handoff automatically: on-device does the fast local work, Claude handles the heavy lifting. Compatible with iOS 27, iPadOS 27, macOS 27, visionOS 27, and watchOS 27, launching tomorrow.
