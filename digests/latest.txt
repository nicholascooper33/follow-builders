No Priors: How Nuclear Will Unlock Energy Abundance with Valor Atomics Founder Isaiah Taylor
https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=5Xvbq_zvOQ4

Takeaway: Valor Atomics just became the first private startup in history to generate nuclear power, and founder Isaiah Taylor's bet is that hardware iteration — not design elegance — is what gives nuclear its long-overdue "Ford moment."

Recorded on-site at Valor's Utah nuclear facility, this episode follows host Sarah Guo through a live tour of the Ward 250, the first advanced reactor ever to produce power from a startup. Taylor, whose great-grandfather was a nuclear physicist on the Manhattan Project, spent a decade watching nuclear startups fail before founding Valor less than three years ago. The company went from incorporation to first criticality in two years and four months, then cut that to seven months for their second reactor. Taylor's goal: a new reactor turning on every few minutes.

The company's thesis is deliberately contrarian. Where most of the nuclear industry consists of "modeling and simulation" companies producing paper reactors with beautiful designs, Valor builds and turns things on. Taylor divides the nuclear startup landscape into two camps — those who think nuclear is a design problem, and those who think it's a hardware execution problem. Valor is firmly in the second camp, and the Ward 250 is their argument.

On safety, Valor also takes a different approach. Rather than reducing the odds of a meltdown through redundant active cooling systems (the failure mode in Three Mile Island and Fukushima), Valor's Triso-fueled, helium-cooled, graphite-moderated reactor is passively safe by physics. During the recording, Taylor describes their planned demonstration: shut off the entire electrical supply to the plant — every safety system at once — and watch decay heat dissipate naturally through convection over two days, with no operator action required.

Valor also built their own reactor protection system after being quoted $5 million and two and a half years by a vendor. Five engineers built a working equivalent in six weeks for $400,000. "Everywhere in nuclear is like this," Taylor says. "It's a fake industry that has not been building anything for forty years, and the little bit that they have been building, they're charging up 100x margin on."

To mark the milestone, Valor powered an NVIDIA Blackwell AI chip directly from their nuclear reactor and hosted a live website — nuclearwebsite.com — that displayed the exact number of uranium atoms split to deliver each page load, along with Valor merchandise only available while the reactor is running.

The long-term vision: as AI converts human labor into energy consumption in manufacturing, the price of energy becomes the price of everything. Taylor believes nuclear can make energy ten times — eventually a thousand times — cheaper, unlocking a future where manufactured goods trend toward free.

Direct quote: "Most of the nuclear industry is a modeling and simulation industry. They're not focused on hardware iteration, hardware execution, building the simplest and safest reactor that allows them to scale. And Valor just demonstrated that we are."
