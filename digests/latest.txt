Training Data — Demis Hassabis on Building DeepMind, AlphaFold, and the Final Stretch to AGI
Published: 2026-04-30
https://www.youtube.com/playlist?list=PLOhHNjZItNnMm5tdW61JpnyxeYH5NDDx8

Takeaway: Demis Hassabis laid out DeepMind's founding vision — "step one, solve intelligence; step two, use it to solve everything else" — and argued the field remains on the trajectory he mapped out in 2009.

In a wide-ranging fireside chat at what appears to be a Sequoia event, Hassabis walked through the origins of DeepMind, his path from chess prodigy to game developer to neuroscientist to AGI researcher, and why he believes AGI is still on track for around 2030. He described the early days when almost no one in academia or industry believed deep learning could lead anywhere: "We almost felt like we were keepers of a secret because no one either in academia or industry really believed that any big progress was possible."

On AI for science, Hassabis explained that AlphaFold was always the intended early proof point, and that Isomorphic Labs is now building on that foundation to automate drug discovery — compressing the average ten-year timeline down to potentially weeks or days by doing nearly all exploration in silico. He sees biology as a natural domain for machine learning because it has "loads of weak signals, weak correlations, tons of data, far too much that any human mind can analyse."

He made a provocative philosophical claim: that information, not energy or matter, is the most fundamental property of the universe — and that this makes AI even more profound than it first appears, since AI is fundamentally about organizing and understanding information.

On consciousness, Hassabis recommended approaching it in two steps: first build an incredibly intelligent and useful tool, then tackle agency and consciousness with that tool as a resource. He also gave his favorite strategy-game teammate answer without hesitation: Von Neumann, "you want a game theorist."

Direct quote: "I wanted to do AI for a long time, so I kind of decided it was the most important thing I could possibly and most interesting thing I could do in my teenage years."

Claude Blog — Redesigning Claude Code on desktop for parallel agents
Published: Apr 14, 2026
https://claude.com/blog/claude-code-desktop-redesign

Anthropic shipped a major redesign of the Claude Code desktop app aimed at developers running multiple agents in parallel. The new experience centers on a sidebar that consolidates all active and recent sessions — you can filter by status, project, or environment, and sessions archive themselves automatically when their PR merges.

Key additions include an integrated terminal, an in-app file editor for spot edits, a faster rebuilt diff viewer, and a drag-and-drop pane layout so you can arrange the workspace however you prefer. A side-chat feature (Cmd/Ctrl + ;) lets you branch off a question mid-task without polluting the main thread's context.

The redesign also brings CLI plugin parity to the desktop app, extends SSH support to Mac, and adds three view modes — Verbose, Normal, and Summary — to dial the interface from full transparency into tool calls down to just results. Available now for Pro, Max, Team, and Enterprise plans.
