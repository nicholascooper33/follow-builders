How Anthropic Uses Claude Fable 5 — with Mike Krieger (Head of Anthropic Labs, co-founder of Instagram)
Hosted by Dan Shipper on the Every podcast.

Takeaway: Mike Krieger, the man who scaled Instagram from a duct-taped server to Facebook-infrastructure scale, now uses Claude Fable as a full-blown engineering teammate — delegating complex overnight tasks, doing architectural planning in conversation, and waking up to finished work.

Krieger joined Dan Shipper to talk about what it's actually like to use Fable day-in and day-out, beyond the first-day impressions. He described a fundamental shift in how he thinks about working with AI — not just faster autocomplete, but genuine task delegation. He now runs multiple concurrent Claude Code sessions in parallel, treating long-horizon background tasks differently from rapid interactive work: "I'll wish Claude a good night, set it up on a pretty complex task, and wake up to... it's usually done by like two in the morning."

One of the more interesting operational insights: Krieger does more upfront architectural planning conversations with Fable now, often asking it to produce HTML diagrams or markdown docs to align his team before anyone writes a line of production code. He says forcing early alignment — even if you prototype first and then back it out into a plan — is the key that lets small teams move fast without accumulating expensive technical debt.

He also talked about model selection as a real daily decision. Fable's extended thinking and effort levels are genuinely wide compared to earlier models, and he found himself consciously dialing down effort for UI tweaks and quick questions — eventually switching his iOS app to Sonnet for day-to-day speed. The honest admission: he felt slightly embarrassed asking Fable an NBA Finals question, then switched apps. The larger product question Krieger raises is whether users should have to think about these choices at all, or whether surface-level defaults (mobile = fast model, desktop = deep work model) should handle it automatically.

Key quote: "It really feels like now it's a teammate that I can delegate a lot of work to."

The conversation is a useful calibration for anyone who thinks they've already maxed out what Fable can do — the consistent message is that the real gains come from extended use, not first impressions.
