No Priors — Scaling Global Organizations in the Age of AI with ServiceNow CEO Bill McDermott
https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=tNNFJa5pUEg

Takeaway: Enterprise workflow platforms and large language models are complementary, not competitive — LLMs think and recommend, platforms act and close cases — and confusing the two is the most expensive mistake an enterprise can make right now.

Bill McDermott joined No Priors host Sarah Guo for a wide-ranging conversation on leadership, the SaaSpocalypse thesis, and what it actually means to build an agentic enterprise. His central argument is both practical and pointed: when a compensation dispute surfaces in a real company, a language model can generate a thoughtful recommendation in milliseconds — but it doesn't close the case. Getting to resolution means routing through HR, finance, legal, and compliance, with full data context and an audit trail. That is what a workflow platform does. He frames this with a clean formula: "AI thinks, but workflow acts. That's an unlock for a lot of people." On cost, he's direct: replicating a mature enterprise platform with net-new LLM-generated code runs roughly 10 times higher once you factor in GPU costs, token pricing, and the labor to rebuild decades of accumulated business logic. And there is a deeper accountability gap LLMs can't close — "People that run businesses understand that people make mistakes. They never will forgive software for making a mistake."

On the SaaSpocalypse narrative, McDermott drew a deliberate parallel to the hyperscaler scare of the previous decade. Everyone expected cloud infrastructure to eat software; instead the hyperscalers became indispensable partners. Language model companies, he argues, will follow the same arc. ServiceNow's strategy is to be the AI control tower that integrates hyperscalers, LLMs, and systems of record into a single workflow fabric — the connective tissue of an agentic enterprise, not a competing layer.

The adoption picture remains uneven. McDermott cited a meeting the night before with 800 Brazilian executives: only 11% of companies in that market have moved past experimentation into mainstream agentic deployment. Financial services firms in the US are moving fastest — rethinking headcount, remodeling operations, chasing expanding margins. ServiceNow itself now handles 90% of customer service cases through AI agents, with humans concentrated on judgment calls and relationship work. He projects 2.2 billion AI agents entering the workforce in the coming years, which substantially raises the bar for what human roles need to offer.

On leadership in a fast-moving environment, McDermott offered a line worth keeping: "It is fast, but it'll never move this slow again. So this is the way it's going to be." His prescription — lean in, be prescriptive with customers, and treat AI as a force multiplier for human ambition.
