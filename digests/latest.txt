No Priors: Pax Silica — Inside the Trump Administration's Tech Strategy with US Under Secretary of State Jacob Helberg
https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xjlYpGaxIPA

The US government is building a private-sector-led coalition to de-risk the AI supply chain from Chinese dominance — and it's moving at "Trump time."

Jacob Helberg, Under Secretary of State for Economic Affairs, joined No Priors to explain Pax Silica, a 14-country economic security coalition designed to secure the inputs America needs for the AI revolution — from rare earth magnets to robotics components to server motors. The centerpiece announcement is a 4,000-acre "economic security zone" in the Philippines, granted to the US State Department as diplomatic property. Over a two-year window, the administration plans to negotiate investor protections and build out a forward-deployed industrial base — a hub specifically targeting the robotics supply chain, which Helberg describes as currently "completely dominated by China."

The strategy's defining difference from China's Belt and Road Initiative is its philosophy: rather than state-owned enterprises building government-operated infrastructure, the US approach puts private companies in the driver's seat. As Helberg put it directly: "We're not gonna do government operated supply chains because that's not how we shine as a country. Our superpower is really our private sector and our companies. American products enchant and delight users around the world by the billions — that is really our edge as a country."

The administration has already held the largest critical minerals summit in State Department history — 55+ countries on February 4th — and signed MOU deals with dozens of nations on mineral security. Helberg said he's confident that pricing issues in the minerals market will be resolved before the end of the administration, calling it central to long-term commercial viability for alternative supply chains.

On AI's economic weight: Helberg cited AI as driving over a third of US GDP growth and pointed to record global demand for copper, cobalt, and data center inputs as evidence of the supply chain pressure building ahead. He called on venture capitalists to help identify top operators in the space, framing VC diligence as a useful signal for government capital allocation decisions.

A June rollout is planned with 4–5 additional major lines of effort. For American founders and builders, Helberg's message was clear: the administration sees the tech ecosystem's risk tolerance, contrarian instincts, and positive-sum ethos as exactly the kind of partnership it is looking to forge.
