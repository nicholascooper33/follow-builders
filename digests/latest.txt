The MAD Podcast with Matt Turck
"The Biggest AI Deployment Nobody Talks About | Samsara CEO Sanjit Biswas"
https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=3FHsGiONOGw

Takeaway: Samsara — the $20B physical operations company — may be running the largest real-world AI deployment on the planet, covering 99% of US roads daily, and their story reveals just how different physical AI is from software AI.

Matt Turck sits down with Sanjit Biswas, cofounder and CEO of Samsara, to dig into what it actually looks like to deploy AI at massive physical scale. Samsara's platform spans GPS trackers, AI dashcams, asset tags, and cloud software that serves millions of frontline workers across trucking, construction, utilities, and logistics. The company crossed $2B ARR, is profitable, growing at 30%, and processes 25 trillion data points a year.

The conversation is a masterclass in why physical AI is harder than software AI. Biswas explains that unlike the digital world — where you can crawl Reddit and tokenize petabytes of existing data — physical operations are largely undigitized. "These are not the tokens you're gonna find online. Like, you can't crawl Reddit and find out about what happened on a construction site."

Samsara's architecture follows a clear three-layer pattern: hardware sensors (dashcams, asset trackers, vehicle gateways) as the eyes and ears; cloud software and AI as the brain; and now agentic systems as the arms. Their most striking recent launch is Agent Studio, debuted at their Beyond 2026 conference in Las Vegas. The flagship demo: a warranty agent that reads a fault code, cross-references OEM warranty agreements and service manuals, determines coverage, opens a work order with repair steps, and flags other affected trucks — all in under a minute. That same task used to take a human one to two hours.

On the question of agents vs. workflows, Biswas gave a clear answer: "I don't think either really works well in isolation." You need agentic reasoning for long-horizon planning, but you still need guardrails and workflow context to keep agents on track.

Biswas also offered a direct quote on their data moat: "We believe we helped prevent about 380,000 car crashes, road accidents in the last year."

The episode closes with a look at the near-term future: AI ride-alongs that coach drivers across an entire 8-hour shift (expensive today, economical soon as token costs fall), mixed human-robot fleets in construction, and a grid modernization boom where one energy utility customer told Biswas they plan to triple in five years the grid capacity built over the last 125 years — with 90% of new demand coming from data centers.
