How Anthropic Uses Claude Fable 5 With Mike Krieger
Show: AI & I by Every

Takeaway: Mike Krieger — head of Anthropic Labs and Instagram co-founder — explains how Fable 5 has forced him to completely rethink what productive work with an AI model looks like, shifting from short prompts to overnight delegation and upfront architectural planning.

Krieger opened by describing the disorienting experience of first encountering Fable 5 internally: "I feel like a total newbie again because I feel like the way that I am prompting or even thinking about decomposing a task is really out of date now with this model." That sense of being reset — even for someone who helped build it — sets the tone for the whole conversation.

The most striking shift Krieger describes is temporal. Where earlier models required constant back-and-forth, Fable 5 has earned enough trust that he now sets it on complex overnight tasks. He recounts waking up to find that Claude had hit a remote service outage mid-task, scaffolded a temporary backend workaround, documented it, and continued — without human intervention. In his words, it "really feels like a teammate that I can delegate a lot of work to."

Krieger has also changed how he plans. He now uses Fable extensively for upfront architectural discussions — often asking it to generate an HTML page or markdown diagram to align his team before writing any code. He links this directly to the pace of AI-assisted development: teams can build so fast now that forcing early alignment has become critical to avoid costly re-work.

On tooling and model selection: Krieger mostly works in the terminal app (Ghostty), while his wife — a UX designer — has taken to Claude Code via the desktop app. He's begun experimenting with effort levels, dialing Fable down to medium for trivial UI tweaks and switching to Sonnet for quick questions where "Fable will go off and think very hard" about something that doesn't warrant it.

The episode closes with a demo of a personal media tracker Krieger built over a weekend — a React Native and web app where Claude can not only search and add content, but actually modify the app itself from within the interface. He called it "agent-native architecture at its fullest degree": every part of the product is accessible via tool calls, and the agent can update the software it's embedded in. A hands-on look at what building with the most capable current model actually feels like from the inside.
