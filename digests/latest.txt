Why the Next Hit AI Product Will Be Social
AI & I by Every | Host: Dan Shipper | Guest: Sarah Tavel, Partner at Benchmark
https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=dlI-5W7d7uU

The big unlock for mass AI adoption isn't a better model — it's the social layer that makes the model accessible, shareable, and learnable from peers.

Sarah Tavel, a Benchmark partner who was early at Pinterest, joined Dan Shipper to argue that every consumer technology paradigm follows the same arc: deeply technical founding teams dominate at the start (Google, OpenAI), then the center of gravity shifts toward product thinkers as the infrastructure matures (Pinterest, Instagram). She sees AI at exactly that inflection point right now.

Her case against current AI products: "Custom GPTs and ChatGPT feels criminal to me. It's clearly made by a team that is unbelievably capable but isn't social." ChatGPT is still essentially a text box — a single-player experience. What made consumer web explosive wasn't just better technology; it was network effects, UGC, and the community of people who showed others what was possible. Pinterest didn't win because it had better image algorithms; it won because people could browse what other people had pinned and learn from taste-makers.

Sarah's prediction: someone will build a UGC-driven AI community where power users surface the best prompts, workflows, and custom setups — dramatically lowering the barrier for everyone else. "Someone's gonna create a UGC type community where there are people who are really, really good, make it so much easier for the rest of us to really take advantage of this technology."

She also flagged DeepSeek as a potential inflection point for AI infrastructure costs, which would accelerate the transition from model-centric to product-centric builders. On the VC side, she's drawn to founders for whom starting a company is an "affliction" — not a career move but a compulsion. "I'm attracted to the founders for whom it's an affliction. It's like this rash that they just have to scratch."

The episode closes with an honest exchange about AI's role in venture decision-making: LLMs are useful for probing thinking, not replacing judgment. The decider still needs to be human — at least for now.
