THE MAD PODCAST WITH MATT TURCK
"OpenAI's Compute Chief: We Can't Build Fast Enough | Sachin Katti"
https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=wEZBlmvxx4o
Published: 2026-07-16

Takeaway: Sachin Katti, OpenAI's Head of Industrial Compute, reveals that demand for AI compute has never once been satisfied — the physical world simply can't build fast enough to keep up, and the internal worry is always undershooting, never overbuilding.

Katti joined OpenAI after stints as a Stanford CS/EE professor, serial founder, and CTO of Intel. His role spans the entire compute lifecycle: finding land, power, and chips; financing the infrastructure; deploying and operating it; and allocating capacity internally. He describes the current data center build-out — with OpenAI planning ~$50B in compute spend this year against an industry-wide $700B — as potentially the largest infrastructure project in human history, bigger than the highway or railroad eras.

On the physical reality: AI data centers are essentially giant supercomputers cooled with liquid rather than air, because the chips run too hot for conventional cooling. Everything from chips to cables to transformers must be cooled. OpenAI builds in rural America (Texas, Michigan, and elsewhere) for land availability, permitting, power access, and construction labor — and always invests in new grid infrastructure when it arrives, so data centers add capacity rather than drain it.

On the Jalapeno custom chip: designed in just nine months — unusually fast — because OpenAI knows exactly what workload the chip needs to run, enabling co-design shortcuts that external vendors can't take. AI itself is now assisting in chip design, accelerating iteration cycles that used to be bottlenecked by human researchers. Katti believes full AI-designed chips are not far off.

On the broader supply picture: inference now equals or exceeds training in compute consumption, because synthetic data generation and post-training are themselves inference. Bottlenecks exist everywhere — gas turbines, transformers, permitting, and a shortage of electricians and plumbers needed to build at this pace. OpenAI's newly announced guaranteed token capacity is a direct response to intelligence becoming a critical supply input for enterprises.

Key quote: "Anytime you have thought you have enough compute, we can slow down — always negatively surprises like, oh, we should not have slowed down. Demand far outstrips compute supply today. So anything we can bring online, we consume immediately."
