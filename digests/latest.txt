AI & I by Every — "The AI Workflows Behind Every's Consulting Team"
Published: July 1, 2026
https://www.youtube.com/playlist?list=PLuMcoKK9mKgHtW_o9h5sGO2vXrffKHwJL

Takeaway: Once AI handles the execution, your job becomes building the systems — not doing the work itself.

Dan Shipper (CEO, Every) sat down with Natalia, Every's head of consulting, to walk through the AI-powered workflows she's built using Codex to transform how she operates — from email triage to eldercare coordination to overnight CRM setup.

The episode's central metaphor lands early and sticks: "Knowledge work now is turning into something like gardening, where when you're gardening, you're creating the conditions for the growth to happen, but you're not like making the plant with your hands."

Natalia manages Claudi, Every's internal AI agent employee, which has its own LinkedIn and social presence, maintains dashboards, and self-evaluates performance through a "trust battery" feedback loop. But she also just hired a human operations person — because AI still needs constant oversight and taste direction, and because clients want to interface with people. The lesson: AI is exceptional at executing against a standard operating procedure, but it still needs managerial support to reach excellence.

Her most striking demo: a 13-hour Codex overnight loop that fully populated a CRM with context drawn from hundreds of emails and conversation notes — weeks of work, done by morning. She also built a family eldercare portal for her 81-year-old father: nurse WhatsApp updates, Google Form health reports, and follow-up action items all centralized in one bilingual (Spanish/English) password-protected site, accessible to family members and caregivers alike.

She also showed her custom email triage app — trained on 150 past emails to write replies in her voice, with one-click actions to send, archive, log to a markdown client file, or push to Asana. Her inbox now knows more about what's going on than she does.

Her advice for executives: start with your existing systems (OKRs, KPIs, shared goals), give that context to AI, then identify what tasks only humans should do. The most common mistake she sees — and makes herself — is trying to rebuild everything at once. Start with one task, write down what doing it well looks like, and build from there.
