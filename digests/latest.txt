Unsupervised Learning, Ep 87: Gemini Co-Lead on World Models, RL's Next Domains & Continual Learning
https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NQczevdpxq0

Takeaway: Oriol Vinyals, co-lead of Gemini alongside Noam Shazir and Jeff Dean, makes the case that world models and memory are the two most underappreciated bets in frontier AI — and explains why Google is uniquely positioned to pursue both.

The conversation was recorded the day after Google IO, making it one of the most timely deep-dives into the research behind the announcements. Jacob Efron (Redpoint investor, host) covered world models, agents, RL, continual learning, and how Google manages research at scale.

On world models: Vinyals explained that world models and code/self-improvement are operating at different layers — they aren't mutually exclusive bets but rather complementary approaches. Google's Omni, shipped at IO, represents a significant step toward what Demis Hassabis has called the path to AGI through world models, though Vinyals was careful not to overstate what current systems can do.

On memory: He argued that advances in memory will look a lot like what reasoning advances looked like over the past few years — foundational and compounding. Memory lets agents carry what they learn forward; the open question is how to keep that memory high-signal as it grows.

On agents: Spark, Google's new consumer agent (an evolution of Project Mariner), resulted from iteratively improving both the model and the scaffolding system around it together. The capability breakthrough came from sequencing — getting the model to a certain level, then building the system, then optimizing both jointly.

On research org design: Vinyals described the tension clearly at frontier labs: research needs some protection from short quarterly cycles, but it can't be too disconnected from where the models actually are. The magic is finding work that's a few steps ahead of the current training run, not years ahead.

Direct quote: "If you're a user, use the models. If you're a builder, use the models to build anything you do, even if you think there's no remote connection to AI. Play with these models. They're amazing, and they will only get better."

Claude Blog — New in Claude Managed Agents: dreaming, outcomes, and multiagent orchestration
https://claude.com/blog/new-in-claude-managed-agents
Published: May 06, 2026

Anthropic shipped three major additions to Claude Managed Agents:

Dreaming (research preview): A scheduled background process that reviews past agent sessions, extracts recurring patterns, and curates memory so agents self-improve over time. Developers can let it update memory automatically or review changes manually. Harvey used it to give their legal drafting agents persistent memory across sessions — including filetype workarounds and tool-specific patterns — and saw completion rates jump roughly 6x in testing.

Outcomes: Developers write a rubric describing what success looks like; a separate grader evaluates output in its own context window (so it's not influenced by the agent's reasoning) and tells the agent what to fix. In testing, outcomes improved task success by up to 10 points, with the biggest gains on the hardest problems. File generation quality also improved: +8.4% on docx, +10.1% on pptx. Spiral by Every is using outcomes to enforce their editorial rubric on every draft before it reaches the user.

Multiagent orchestration: A lead agent can now break complex jobs into pieces and delegate to specialist subagents with their own models, prompts, and tools, running in parallel on a shared filesystem. Every step is traceable in the Claude Console. Netflix's platform team is using this to analyze logs from hundreds of builds in parallel and surface only the patterns worth acting on.
