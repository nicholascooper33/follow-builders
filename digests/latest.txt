No Priors — Baseten CEO Tuhin Srivastava on the AI Inference Crunch, Custom Models, and Building the Inference Cloud

The key takeaway: AI inference is the last market standing — even in a post-AGI world, everything still runs on inference, and we are nowhere near having enough compute to meet demand.

Tuhin Srivastava, founder and CEO of Baseten, joined No Priors hosts Sarah Guo and Elad Gil to discuss what it looks like to grow 30x in a year, operate 90 clusters across 18 clouds, and hold a daily 4pm capacity meeting as a permanent fixture of company culture. Baseten is on track to exceed $1B in revenue this year.

Baseten serves some of the fastest-growing AI-native companies — Abridge, Cursor, Open Evidence, Decagon — and Srivastava's core thesis is that the application layer survives commoditization because companies that gather unique user signal and encode it in workflows (not just models) build durable moats. "To the extent that it is encoded in workflows, that is where they will be able to develop moat," he said. His concrete example: Abridge's ambient scribe product, so deeply integrated into hospital workflows and EMRs, is nearly impossible for a frontier lab to replicate without access to the same clinical user signal.

On model mix, Srivastava revealed a striking stat: 95% of tokens served on Baseten today are from custom models, not vanilla open source weights. Nearly every customer modifies the base model with their own data — for quality, performance, or specialized use cases. Post-training expertise is now so central to the business that Baseten acquired a research team (Parzed) to offer it as a product.

The supply crunch is real and worse than most people realize. Baseten runs clusters at "uncomfortably high utilization" — mid-nineties — and quality of compute supply is as much a problem as quantity: "There's probably like a dozen good clouds, and I'd probably put three or four of them in the gold tier." Buying future capacity now requires 3–5 year contracts with 20–30% TCV prepayments, which is pushing Baseten to think seriously about accessing public markets for lower cost of capital.

On Jevons paradox, Srivastava is bullish: cheaper inference doesn't cannibalize demand — it amplifies it. "If you make it cheaper, they'll insert a hell of a lot more intelligence." Longer-running agents are the direct result of inference costs coming down. The episode closes with a vision of AI as personalized concierge for healthcare, education, and daily life — and a belief that software engineering headcount isn't shrinking, it's just building exponentially more things.

Anthropic Engineering — An update on recent Claude Code quality reports
https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/april-23-postmortem

Anthropic published a detailed postmortem on three separate changes that degraded Claude Code quality over the past month. The issues: (1) a default reasoning effort downgrade from high to medium, reverted April 7 after users reported feeling less intelligent responses; (2) a caching bug that cleared Claude's thinking history every single turn instead of just once after an idle session, causing forgetfulness and unusual tool choices — fixed April 10; and (3) a system prompt change capping responses to 25 words between tool calls, which hurt coding quality and was reverted April 20. All three have been resolved as of v2.1.116. Anthropic is resetting usage limits for all subscribers and committing to broader eval suites, tighter system prompt controls, and a new ClaudeDevs X account for transparent product communication.
