Unsupervised Learning (Redpoint AI) — Ep 88: Unpacking DeepMind's Quest for SuperIntelligence with Demis Hassabis' Biographer
Published: June 1, 2026
https://www.youtube.com/@RedpointAI

Takeaway: Sebastian Mallaby, author of The Infinity Machine, argues that Demis Hassabis and DeepMind have been consistently underestimated by the industry — and that the AI safety problem is now fundamentally a collective action problem that only governments can solve.

Sebastian Mallaby spent over 30 hours with Demis Hassabis in preparation to write The Infinity Machine, a biography tracing DeepMind from its 2010 founding through the current AI race. The conversation covers the full arc of Demis's thinking on safety, competition, and what drives him.

On the race dynamic: Mallaby traces today's competitive chaos to a 2015 DeepMind safety summit hosted at SpaceX, where Elon Musk was invited to become an inside ally. Instead, he used what he learned to co-found OpenAI. The lesson Demis drew: individual trust between labs can't hold, and the only path to real safety coordination is government enforcement — ideally a US-China agreement, "remote though that prospect may seem." He has shifted from hoping for a "singleton scenario" (one responsible lab steering toward AGI) to seeing safety as a collective action problem.

On government capacity: Mallaby pushes back on cynicism. The UK AI Safety Institute, led by Geoffrey Irving (formerly of both DeepMind and OpenAI), has already found model vulnerabilities that the private labs missed and quietly shared them — evidence that public institutions can attract serious talent when the mission is compelling. He draws a parallel to the FDA: if we can peer-review drugs before release, we should be able to peer-review AI models.

On DeepMind's competitive position: Mallaby argues people were too quick to crown OpenAI and dismissed both Demis personally and Google DeepMind as a company. Gemini 3.0 topped leaderboards in late 2025, only to be displaced by Anthropic's recent surge. The deeper pattern is that DeepMind's "try everything" research culture — rooted in Demis's broad intellectual formation — made it slower to take concentrated product bets. Anthropic got to coding leadership precisely because it chose not to pursue generative video or other adjacent areas.

Direct quote: "He would sometimes erupt in these conversations I was having, and he would start banging the table and saying, maybe if we approach science the right way, we understand more about nature, we will be getting closer to something that we could perhaps call God."

The last line of Mallaby's book is a direct Demis quote: "I'm optimistic still."
