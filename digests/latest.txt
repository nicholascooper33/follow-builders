The MAD Podcast with Matt Turck — "OpenAI's Compute Chief: We Can't Build Fast Enough | Sachin Katti"
Published: July 16, 2026
https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=wEZBlmvxx4o

Takeaway: The biggest risk for OpenAI isn't overbuilding compute — it's not building fast enough, because demand has consistently outpaced every supply forecast.

Sachin Katti, OpenAI's Head of Industrial Compute, sat down with Matt Turck at the RACE conference in Paris for one of the most grounded interviews yet on the physical infrastructure behind the AI boom. Katti came to OpenAI from Intel (where he was CTO) and Stanford (where he was a CS and EE professor), and brings an unusually full-stack view: research, startups, semiconductors, and now the largest infrastructure build in human history.

The conversation opens with a striking reframe. Most AI discourse focuses on models and software, but Katti describes data centers as "giant factories that are turning electrons into tokens" — football-field-scale facilities filled with liquid-cooled supercomputers, where everything that processes energy produces heat that must be removed. Liquid cooling, long known in high-performance computing, is being deployed at unprecedented scale, with active innovation in new materials and fluids that improve heat transfer efficiency — because hotter chips mean more memory bandwidth and higher FLOP counts.

On power: early data centers plugged into the grid, but at today's scale OpenAI is actively funding new generation and transmission infrastructure wherever it builds. Katti frames this as a net positive for communities — rural areas get new property tax revenue, upgraded grid infrastructure, and jobs that wouldn't otherwise exist. For sites that can't draw enough grid power, behind-the-meter gas turbines are the current fallback. Nuclear, he says, "can't come soon enough."

The most forward-looking thread was on AI designing its own hardware. OpenAI's custom chip, Jalapeno (built with Broadcom), went from design to tape-out in nine months — record speed Katti attributes partly to the team's TPU experience, partly to Broadcom's track record, and partly to a unique advantage: knowing the exact future workload meant they could co-design chip and model together. "We do believe that the world of recursion is not that far where AI will design the systems it needs to train and run the next generation of AI."

On the overbuilding debate: Katti is unambiguous. "Anytime we have thought we have enough compute, we can slow down — always negatively surprises like, oh, we should not have slowed down." Every time compute comes online, it gets consumed immediately. The worry runs the other direction: supply chains, factories, and grid infrastructure don't scale as fast as software demand.

Anthropic Engineering — "An update on recent Claude Code quality reports"
https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/april-23-postmortem

A detailed post-mortem on three separate bugs that combined to produce what looked like broad, inconsistent degradation in Claude Code over the past month. The three issues: (1) a March 4 change dropped default reasoning effort from high to medium to reduce latency — wrong tradeoff, reverted April 7; (2) a caching optimization shipped March 26 had a bug that caused it to strip prior reasoning on every turn of a session, not just once — making Claude appear forgetful and draining usage limits faster than expected, fixed April 10; (3) a system prompt addition on April 16 limiting response length hurt coding quality and was reverted April 20. All three are now resolved. As of April 23, Anthropic reset usage limits for all subscribers. Going forward: broader eval suites for every system prompt change, tighter rollout controls, and more internal staff using the exact public build.

Anthropic Engineering — "Scaling Managed Agents: Decoupling the brain from the hands"
https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/managed-agents

A technical deep-dive on the architecture behind Claude Managed Agents. The core problem: putting the agent harness, session log, and sandbox all in one container creates a "pet" — a fragile, named individual that's hard to debug and impossible to lose. The solution: decouple the brain (Claude + harness) from the hands (sandbox/tools) and the session (event log), making each an interface with well-defined boundaries. This dropped p50 time-to-first-token by ~60% and p95 by over 90%, since containers are only provisioned when actually needed. Security also improved: credentials no longer live in the same environment as Claude's generated code. The post draws an explicit parallel to how operating systems virtualized hardware into abstractions that outlasted the hardware itself — the goal is interfaces general enough to accommodate Claude harnesses that don't exist yet.

Claude Blog — "New in Claude Managed Agents: self-hosted sandboxes and MCP tunnels"
Published: May 19, 2026
https://claude.com/blog/claude-managed-agents-updates

Two new capabilities for Claude Managed Agents. Self-hosted sandboxes let agent tool execution run on infrastructure you control — code, files, services, and data stay within your enterprise perimeter while Anthropic handles the orchestration layer. Supported providers include Cloudflare, Daytona, Modal, and Vercel. MCP tunnels let agents connect to private Model Context Protocol servers inside your network without public endpoints — a lightweight gateway makes a single outbound connection, no inbound firewall rules required. Both features are available now (self-hosted sandboxes in public beta, MCP tunnels in research preview).
