AI & I by Every — "How Every's Team Used AI to Ship Its Biggest Launch Ever"
Published: 2026-07-22 | https://www.youtube.com/playlist?list=PLuMcoKK9mKgHtW_o9h5sGO2vXrffKHwJL

Takeaway: Every's team used Claude Code, Codex, and a compound agentic loop to execute their biggest subscription revenue launch in company history — automating the marketing execution entirely while the team focused on ideas and judgment.

Every launched "All Access" — a $625/year tier anchored by the Builder Pack, which bundles exclusive access to 10 AI tools the team uses daily: Cursor, Claude Max, Codex, Framer, PostHog, Spiral, and others. The launch generated the largest subscription revenue increase in Every's history.

The standout story from the episode: Austin spotted a Slack conversation suggesting they send targeted follow-up emails to users who'd shown checkout intent but hadn't converted. Rather than sit down at his computer, he took a screenshot of the Slack thread, dropped it into a Codex thread using Claude 5.6 on medium effort, typed "can you do this?" and then invoked /LFG — a compound engineering harness in Every's internal Claude Code setup that runs an agent through brainstorm → plan → work → review → loop cycles.

Then he went to the gym.

When he came back, the agent had: segmented users into four distinct audience cohorts based on checkout behavior; drafted tailored email copy for each cohort; run the copy through Spiral (Every's AI writing tool) to match house voice; pulled a high-performing social image from a previous send; and scheduled all four campaigns for the next morning. Austin made light tweaks — "partially because I'm still a control freak who doesn't want to turn over every single thing to AI" — spending maybe ten minutes total. The emails went out and performed.

Other team members share their own approaches. Yash (who runs Sparkle and works on growth) has automated his A/B testing pipeline almost entirely through a Claude + PostHog loop, freeing him to focus on what experiments to run rather than how to set them up: "The only way I can do more experiments is automating the entire pipeline." He describes Claude Max as deeply underrated: "I'd pay way more for it. I couldn't work without it."

A theme throughout: the people at Every who are thriving with AI are the ones comfortable letting go — automating the boring execution so they can stay in the idea and judgment layer. The Builder Pack is their bet that other builders want the same stack.

Direct quote: "I took a screenshot of the Slack. I dropped it in a Codex thread... I said, can you do this? And then I did slash LFG... And then I went to the gym, and I came back from the gym, and what I saw in Codex was that it works for honestly not that long... And it had created four different audience based cohorts." — Austin, Every team
