Drawing on experience inside frontier model launch reviews, Guru explains why shipping an LLM is fundamentally unlike shipping traditional software: you're making a judgment call about a black box with effectively infinite use cases and failure modes. No Priors — Biohub: The Future of Biology is Open-Source
With Mark Zuckerberg, Priscilla Chan, and Alex Rives, Head of Science at Biohub

Takeaway: Zuckerberg and Chan are betting $500M that frontier AI and frontier biology need to be built together — and that open-source tooling is the fastest path to curing disease.

The episode traces the decade-long arc of the Chan Zuckerberg Initiative's science work, from early single-cell sequencing grants to the current Virtual Biology Initiative. The core thesis: biology has the same structural problems that open-source software solved for engineering — siloed knowledge, tools that live on one postdoc's laptop and disappear when they graduate, and no shared infrastructure for the field to build on. Biohub's answer has been long-term tool development, bringing engineers and scientists together across universities.

What's changed is the AI piece. Priscilla Chan describes the moment large language models arrived as the unlock they'd been waiting for: "What if we could actually understand how biology worked? Move it from a discovery-based science to an engineering-based science where we could systematically understand how living cells worked and be able to understand why things go wrong."

Alex Rives — who left Evolutionary Scale (where he'd assembled a team and raised venture funding for biological foundation models) to lead science at Biohub — explains the appeal: "To build that next institution for the next era, you would really need to have frontier artificial intelligence. You would have to have frontier biology. You would need to start to put those things in feedback and really have models that are learning from the biology."

The team has already folded over 1.1 billion proteins and predicted their structures. The next horizon is cell-level and whole-system modeling — creating datasets that don't yet exist by building new imaging, measurement, and cellular engineering tools across the San Francisco, New York, and Chicago Biohub locations. Unlike language models trained on internet text, biology models need novel scientific data generation, not just curation.

A key quote from Zuckerberg: "We'll have a bigger impact by getting tools in more scientists' hands quicker by doing it as open-source projects instead."
