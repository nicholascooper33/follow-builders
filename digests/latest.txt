AI & I by Every — Best of the Pod: Wired's Kevin Kelly on Why AI Is a 50-year Overnight Success

Takeaway: Kevin Kelly argues we understand AI the way Isaac Newton understood electricity — we can use it, but we're profoundly wrong about what it actually is.

Kevin Kelly, Wired co-founder and author of The Inevitable, joined Dan Shipper for a wide-ranging conversation about technology waves, creative work, and the nature of intelligence itself.

Kelly's most striking claim is that intelligence isn't a single thing — it's more like chemistry. Just as there are elements and compounds, there will be cognitive elements and cognitive compounds. AI mixture-of-experts architectures are already beginning to do this, combining different types of reasoning. "We haven't done the chemistry of identifying what some of the basic cognitive units are," he said. He draws a direct parallel to electricity: Newton, one of the smartest people who ever lived, was "totally wrong about electricity" when it was first encountered. We're in the same position with intelligence today.

Kelly also introduced a concept he calls the "audience of one" — 50 million AI-generated images are created daily, with 99.999% made for the pleasure of the co-creator alone. He built out an entire epic historical fantasy novel (da Vinci, Columbus, and Martin Luther stuck in a hotel together, founding a science city in the New World) and described the act of generating it as more pleasurable than reading it. "There's no economic model for the audience of one," he admits — except for OpenAI.

On VR, he notes that it remains stuck: "VR is still waiting for its LLM moment." The hardware of 40 years ago isn't much worse than today's — it just got a million times cheaper. What VR needs is an unexpected technical breakthrough, possibly from a completely different direction, the way LLMs emerged from work on language translation rather than reasoning directly.

Kelly's advice for navigating the frontier: the best futurists are also great historians. He balances reading about AI with deep historical research and working with his hands. He is "a nomad" of the frontier — visiting the edge to report back, but not living there.

Direct quote: "AI is a fifty year overnight success. And VR is still waiting for its LLM moment."

Claude Blog — Building intelligent apps for Apple platforms with Claude in the Foundation Models framework

Anthropic released a new Swift package that lets Apple developers use Apple's Foundation Models framework to call Claude for complex workflows. The integration works on iOS 27, iPadOS 27, macOS 27, visionOS 27, and watchOS 27. Apple's on-device models handle fast local tasks like summarization; when a request needs multi-step reasoning, code generation, or web search, the app hands off to Claude. The package handles streaming, tool calls, and structured responses back into SwiftUI views. Because Apple's framework returns typed Swift values, developers arrive at the Claude API call with clean inputs rather than raw text.
