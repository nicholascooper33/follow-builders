Unsupervised Learning — Ep 87: Gemini Co-Lead Oriol Vinyals on World Models, RL's Next Domains & Continual Learning
https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NQczevdpxq0

Takeaway: Oriol Vinyals, co-lead of Gemini alongside Noam Shazeer and Jeff Dean, makes the case that the next paradigm shift in AI will look less like a new architecture and more like memory — specifically, agents that accumulate and refine knowledge across sessions the way humans do.

The conversation, recorded the day after Google IO 2026, covers a lot of ground. On world models, Vinyals explains that Gemini Omni is less about classical representation learning (compressing video into latent concepts) and more about giving users a language-steerable renderer of the world — think: "move forward" as input, simulated physics as output. He's candid that the "GPT moment" for pure visual-only understanding (no text labels) hasn't arrived yet, and that deriving something like the rules of gravity purely from video data remains an open research challenge.

On RL and what domains come next, Vinyals made a notable admission: "Training on narrow points of great difficulty like maths or coding creates this generalization. I think that is not something I quite predicted to work as well as it did." He's now more interested in what he calls "meta capabilities" — not coding or math performance, but the ability to learn from experience and adapt, tested via novel in-context games the model has never seen. His favorite eval: give a model the instruction manual for a game like Civilization, then watch whether it can learn to play better as it plays.

On memory and continual learning, Vinyals argues the near-term path isn't about writing new knowledge into model weights (too hard to serve at scale) but about sophisticated file-system-style external memory — agents writing structured notes to disk that can be retrieved and updated. He sees this as potentially paradigm-shifting in the same way reasoning models were a year and a half ago. Dreaming-style memory consolidation (reviewing past sessions to extract patterns) is the mechanism he finds most promising.

On AGI: "If seven years ago I had to experiment with a model that we have currently, would I have declared this is AGI? I would say probably yes." He thinks genuine scientific innovation — not just executing known recipes, but generating genuinely novel ideas with taste — remains the clearest unsolved capability.

Direct quote: "The ability to continually learn or learn from experience very efficiently... that is a capability that I can sort of measure or feel, and probably it's not super super good yet."

Claude Blog — New in Claude Managed Agents: dreaming, outcomes, and multiagent orchestration
https://claude.com/blog/new-in-claude-managed-agents

Anthropic shipped three significant additions to Claude Managed Agents. Dreaming is a scheduled background process that reviews past agent sessions and memory stores, extracts patterns across runs (including recurring mistakes and converged workflows), and restructures memory to stay high-signal over time — with the option for developers to review changes before they land. Outcomes lets developers write a rubric for success; a separate grader evaluates the agent's output against that rubric in its own context window and tells the agent what to fix, repeating until the bar is cleared. In internal testing, outcomes improved task success by up to 10 points over standard prompting loops. Multiagent orchestration allows a lead agent to break work into pieces and delegate to specialists with their own models, prompts, and tools — running in parallel on a shared filesystem, with full step-by-step tracing in the Claude Console.

Real deployments: Harvey uses dreaming to help legal agents remember filetype workarounds and tool patterns between sessions, with ~6x completion rate improvement. Netflix's platform team uses multiagent orchestration to analyze logs from hundreds of builds in parallel and surface only cross-cutting patterns. Spiral by Every uses multiagent + outcomes for its writing agent: a Haiku lead agent delegates to Opus subagents for drafting, and each draft is scored against an editorial rubric before being returned to the user.
