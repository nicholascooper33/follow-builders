Ep 92: xAI Co-Founder Unpacks the Future of Model Development
Unsupervised Learning with Jacob Efron | Guest: Igor Babushkin (co-founder of xAI, now founder of River AI)
https://www.youtube.com/@RedpointAI

Takeaway: Igor Babushkin — who led StarCraft and AlphaCode at DeepMind, did early reasoning research at OpenAI, and built the Colossus supercluster at xAI — explains why he left to start River AI, and what he believes the AI industry is getting wrong about how to distribute the benefits of AI.

Igor opens with a vivid description of the December 2025 inflection point when coding agents became impossible to ignore: "It was a real transformation for all of us... you get this incredible sense of anything is possible now." He frames the moment using the Sorcerer's Apprentice metaphor — powerful tools that are increasingly hard to contain, and a need to think carefully about what comes next. The key question he's chasing: what does the AI transformation look like beyond coding, which succeeded because it's verifiable? His candidates include scientific discovery (using tools like Lean to formalize mathematical proofs), material science (closing the loop with real-world experiments), and everyday personal AI for consumers who will never need a frontier reasoning model.

River AI is organized around three bets. First, the River API: a reinforcement learning and fine-tuning service aimed at helping companies take control of AI, optimized for cheapness, reliability, and scale. Second, personalization: breaking the assumption that one model should behave the same way for all users — "your AI agent will speak differently to you than my AI agent speaks to me." Third, local hardware: bringing frontier inference to a device that sits in your home or office, eliminating the API gatekeeping, reducing latency for voice and video applications, and giving individuals actual data sovereignty.

The policy motivation runs through all three. As Igor puts it: "Power over AI is concentrating quite a bit. And especially in the US you can feel that people aren't too happy about this... I think it's important we show some ways in which people can feel the benefits, can feel like they're in control." He views distributing AI access not just as a product opportunity but as a form of AI safety — the most urgent one.

The episode is worth a full listen for anyone thinking about what the post-coding-agent era looks like: what new domains become unlocked, why data availability is the next hard bottleneck for science agents, and why Igor thinks closed-source model providers are in a structurally difficult position as businesses.

Claude Code now supports Artifacts — Claude Blog (published Jun 18, 2026)
https://claude.com/blog/artifacts-in-claude-code

Claude Code sessions can now capture work as live, shareable visual pages called Artifacts — PR walkthroughs, incident timelines, dashboards, release checklists — that update in place as the session progresses. The artifact is built from the full context of the session: your codebase, connected monitoring tools, conversation history. When a new version is published, the page refreshes at the same URL for anyone already viewing it, with full version history. A common internal use case at Anthropic: an engineer kicks off an incident investigation before standup, Claude publishes a timeline with suspect commits and error-rate charts, republishes it twice as the investigation deepens, and the team arrives at standup already oriented. Available in beta to Claude Team and Enterprise orgs from the CLI and desktop app.
