No Priors — "Chasing Trillion-Dollar Companies, Founder Ambition, Token Budgets, and Regulatory Capture with Sarah & Elad"
https://www.youtube.com/@NoPriorsPodcast
Published: August 6, 2026

Takeaway: The trillion-dollar company wave of 2020–2025 was a historically unusual inflection point — and founders, investors, and lab researchers are all misreading what comes next.

Sarah Guo (Conviction) and Elad Gil open by challenging the assumption that several new trillion-dollar companies will emerge in the next 3–5 years. Elad points out that SpaceX and Google each took 15–20 years to reach that scale. The recent sprint by OpenAI, Anthropic, and SpaceX was the exception, not the norm. "A lot of people now are looking at different areas that are very exciting, very promising areas, robotics, materials — and everything in everybody's mind is going to be a trillion dollar company." He's skeptical: even reaching $50–100B in revenue — the threshold needed to justify a trillion-dollar valuation — is a very short list of markets.

A significant thread is what Elad calls "Return on Invested Tokens": the emerging question inside top labs of which researchers and projects deserve outsized compute allocations. Labs are now hiring researchers only above a very high bar because "the cost isn't the researcher, it's the compute associated with the person." This leads to a counterintuitive take on SaaS: rather than dying, SaaS may persist because companies won't want to burn expensive tokens on internal tooling when those same tokens could go toward core product outcomes.

The most striking exchange concerns recursive self-improvement (RSI). Elad notes that some people at the labs believe code will be "completely done, like, as a solved problem" by end of this year, with light RSI by end of 2027. That belief is producing manic 16-hour workdays and genuine existential questions among researchers. Sarah calls it "psychologically most similar to if people think they're gonna die." Elad's reply: "You should get married. You should go ahead. It'll be okay." Sarah adds a sobering note: "I think the reactions of some really extraordinary research friends to it, it feels a little bit tragic."

The episode closes on regulatory capture. Elad uses nuclear power as the cautionary tale: "70% of France is still nuclear in terms of its power generation. 70%. Where are all the accidents and where are all the kerfuffles? Nothing's happened. US is 18%, and we haven't built a reactor in forty years. We had a safety lobby in the seventies basically kill abundant clean energy for us. There are real outcomes where safety has hurt us." Both hosts are alert to a similar dynamic playing out with AI safety regulation — not arguing against safety, but against a framework that counts only risks and never benefits.
